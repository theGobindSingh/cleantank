## 1. Purpose of This Section

This section defines how Clean Tank behaves when nobody is watching, when work becomes difficult, and when competing priorities create pressure.

These values are not decorative statements. They are operating principles for how Clean Tank people work, make decisions, solve problems, serve customers, work with one another, and improve the way the company operates.

The test is simple:

> **If a value cannot be seen in someone's behaviour, it is not yet a Clean Tank value.**

These values should therefore be used in:

- hiring and interview decisions;
- onboarding and training;
- daily supervision and coaching;
- performance conversations and reviews;
- customer-service decisions;
- operational and safety decisions;
- recognition and appreciation;
- handling mistakes and repeated negligence;
- evaluating process improvements;
- resolving disagreements;
- deciding what to do when time, cost, quality, safety and customer expectations conflict.

# 2. The Clean Tank Values at a Glance

| Value | Core idea |
|---|---|
| **1. Right Work, Right Way** | Do the work to the required standard, through the right process, safely and completely. |
| **2. Own the Outcome** | Do not stop at “my task is done”; take responsibility for helping achieve the right overall result. |
| **3. Keep the Promise** | Treat commitments seriously, communicate realistically, and never make promises beyond authority. |
| **4. Respect People, Build the Team** | Treat people with dignity, help each other grow, share knowledge, and correct with respect. |
| **5. Stay Disciplined, Keep Improving** | Follow time, SOPs and professional standards while continuously finding better ways to work. |

### The non-negotiable boundary

> **Quality and Safety are never sacrificed for speed, cost, workload or customer pressure.**

This is not a sixth value. It is the boundary within which all five values operate.

# 3. How to Use These Values

A Clean Tank value should answer a practical question:

> **“What should I do in this situation?”**

When values appear to compete, use this sequence:

1. **Protect Safety.**
2. **Protect the required Quality standard.**
3. **Take ownership of the outcome.**
4. **Honour the customer commitment within the agreed scope and authority.**
5. **Work as a team and communicate respectfully.**
6. **Maintain discipline and improve the process where possible.**

This does not mean every decision follows a rigid formula. It means that speed, convenience, margin or hierarchy must not be used as excuses to bypass safety or required quality.

# 4. VALUE 01 — RIGHT WORK, RIGHT WAY

## Motto

> **“Do the work right. Do it the right way.”**

## What it means

Clean Tank does not define quality only by whether the final result looks acceptable.

The right result must come from the right process, appropriate safety practices, consistent standards, complete execution and proper handover.

For Clean Tank, **quality is the whole experience of doing the work well** — the process, the people, the execution, the customer experience and the final handover.

“Good enough” is not the target when a better result is reasonably achievable within the available time and resources.

At the same time, Clean Tank does not confuse quality with unlimited perfection. The required standard is non-negotiable; improvement beyond that standard must remain practical and responsible.

## What it looks like

- Follow the approved SOP and required process rather than taking shortcuts for convenience.
- Complete required steps even when the customer is pushing for faster completion.
- Maintain a consistent standard from one employee or site to another.
- Treat safety as part of doing the work correctly.
- Inspect work rather than assuming it is complete.
- If the result does not meet the required standard, take action to correct it.
- Treat customer concerns seriously and inspect the concern before responding.
- If the work is technically correct but the customer experience is poor, treat that as a quality issue too.
- Leave the site properly after the work: equipment, waste, cleanliness and handover are part of completion.
- Explain proactively to the customer what was done, what was checked and what they need to know next.
- If a better method is discovered, evaluate it rather than informally changing the process.
- When an approved SOP does not fit an unusual site situation, use judgement to identify a solution — but never make an unapproved deviation where safety may be affected.
- If a new method proves better, safer or more effective, validate and approve it before making it part of the standard process.

## What it does NOT mean

- It does **not** mean sacrificing safety to achieve a better result.
- It does **not** mean spending unlimited time or money chasing perfection.
- It does **not** mean skipping required steps because “the customer will not notice.”
- It does **not** mean accepting inconsistent quality because “everyone has their own way.”
- It does **not** mean treating an acceptable standard as an excuse to stop careless work.
- It does **not** mean blindly following an SOP when a genuine problem exists; improvement must happen through a controlled process.

## Decision rule

> **Meet the required standard every time. Improve beyond it when practical. Never compromise safety to do so.**

## Interview scenario

> **“Tell me about a time when you could have completed a job faster by skipping a step. What did you do, and why?”**

Follow-up:

> “What would you do if the customer was pressuring you to finish immediately?”

## Coaching question

> **“Was the job only completed, or was it completed properly?”**

# 5. VALUE 02 — OWN THE OUTCOME

## Motto

> **“My task is not the finish line. The result is.”**

## What it means

Clean Tank employees are expected to think beyond their assigned task.

Finishing one’s own activity is not enough if the overall customer or team result is still wrong.

Ownership means noticing problems, taking initiative, helping others, coordinating across roles, solving what can reasonably be solved, and escalating correctly when authority or resources are required.

Ownership does **not** mean taking blame for someone else's mistake. It means taking responsibility for helping solve the problem.

## What it looks like

- If you see a problem, do not automatically ask, “Whose responsibility is this?”
- If your assigned work is complete but a teammate is struggling, help them.
- If another worker is using an unsafe or incorrect method, respectfully intervene and escalate when necessary.
- If the overall customer result is poor even though your individual task was correct, help find the cause and solution.
- Take initiative when an important task has no clear owner and could otherwise be missed.
- When authority or resources are needed, approach the right person and keep the matter moving.
- Coordinate with other teams instead of hiding behind department boundaries.
- If you make a mistake, first try to contain or correct the impact, then report meaningful issues appropriately.
- When a problem is outside your authority, provide management with the facts and a useful recommendation before handing it over.
- Do not leave an issue at “I informed someone”; follow it far enough to ensure it has been properly handed over.

## What it does NOT mean

- It does **not** mean making decisions outside your authority.
- It does **not** mean taking blame for mistakes you did not make.
- It does **not** mean working without regard to safety, resources or reasonable limits.
- It does **not** mean bypassing management when approval is required.
- It does **not** mean “ownership” is an excuse for unlimited unpaid work.

## Decision rule

> **Take the problem as far as you responsibly can. When authority is required, make a complete handover — not an abandonment.**

## Interview scenario

> **“Tell me about a time when your own task was complete but the overall result was still at risk. What did you do?”**

Follow-up:

> “What if the problem was not your fault?”

## Coaching question

> **“Is the task done, or is the outcome actually right?”**

# 6. VALUE 03 — KEEP THE PROMISE

## Motto

> **“A commitment creates responsibility.”**

## What it means

Clean Tank takes commitments seriously.

A commitment is not simply something said to make a customer happy. It is a responsibility that must be planned, communicated and delivered responsibly.

Keeping a promise does not mean saying yes to everything. It means making realistic commitments, staying accountable to them, communicating early when circumstances change, and never making promises beyond one’s authority.

Customer commitment is important, but it never overrides Clean Tank's Quality or Safety standards.

## What it looks like

- Treat agreed customer commitments as real responsibilities.
- Before promising something, understand the scope, resources, timeline and authority involved.
- Never promise something beyond your authority.
- If a verbal customer request is outside your authority, seek management confirmation before committing.
- If circumstances threaten a deadline, inform management early and work on a realistic solution.
- Communicate customer updates through the authorised channel.
- If the agreed scope and customer expectation differ, clarify the difference calmly and find an approved solution.
- If a customer makes an unreasonable demand, maintain the relationship respectfully while explaining the boundary and offering a practical alternative.
- Make reasonable extra effort when a genuine commitment requires it.
- Never hide a developing problem simply to make a commitment appear on track.

## What it does NOT mean

- It does **not** mean saying “yes” to every customer request.
- It does **not** mean compromising safety or quality to meet a deadline.
- It does **not** mean making unauthorised promises.
- It does **not** mean hiding problems until the last moment.
- It does **not** mean giving away unapproved extra work simply to avoid an uncomfortable conversation.
- It does **not** mean blaming the customer when expectations were unclear; clarify and solve.

## Decision rule

> **Keep the promise when you can. When you cannot, surface the problem early, get the right decision, and communicate realistically.**

## Interview scenario

> **“Tell me about a commitment you made that later became difficult to deliver. What did you do?”**

Follow-up:

> “Would you ever tell a customer ‘yes’ if you knew you did not have authority to make that promise?”

# 7. VALUE 04 — RESPECT PEOPLE, BUILD THE TEAM

## Motto

> **“Respect people. Help people grow. Win together.”**

## What it means

Clean Tank believes the quality of the team directly affects the quality of the result.

Respect means speaking appropriately, preserving people's dignity, listening to their ideas, and allowing people to disagree without humiliation.

Teamwork means helping others, teaching, sharing knowledge, appreciating good work, and correcting mistakes constructively.

Hierarchy matters for responsibility and authority, but **knowledge and good ideas do not belong to a designation**.

## What it looks like

### With customers and seniors

- Speak respectfully and professionally.
- Listen before responding.
- Disagree respectfully when facts or safety require it.
- Never use authority as an excuse for disrespect.

### With juniors and teammates

- Be friendly, approachable and supportive.
- Teach rather than humiliate.
- Correct privately rather than embarrassing someone publicly.
- Help a teammate who is struggling.
- Share useful knowledge and techniques with the team.
- Encourage quieter team members to contribute.
- Appreciate good work openly.
- Acknowledge when a junior or teammate has a better idea.
- Resolve disagreements using facts rather than hierarchy.
- Escalate professionally when a disagreement begins affecting work.
- Keep personal disagreements from damaging professional responsibilities.

### When someone makes a mistake

- First understand what happened.
- Help correct the problem.
- Explain how to avoid repeating it.
- If the same negligence continues after clear feedback, appropriate disciplinary action may follow.

## What it does NOT mean

- It does **not** mean avoiding accountability to “keep everyone happy.”
- It does **not** mean tolerating repeated negligence.
- It does **not** mean blind obedience to seniority.
- It does **not** mean correcting someone publicly.
- It does **not** mean hiding a team member's serious mistake.
- It does **not** mean individual performers can hold back knowledge to protect their importance.

## Decision rule

> **Correct privately. Appreciate openly. Listen regardless of designation. Escalate when necessary.**

## Interview scenario

> **“Tell me about a time you had to correct someone less experienced than you. How did you handle it?”**

Follow-up:

> “Tell me about a time a junior person had a better idea than you.”

# 8. VALUE 05 — STAY DISCIPLINED, KEEP IMPROVING

## Motto

> **“Respect the standard. Improve the standard.”**

## What it means

Clean Tank expects discipline in everyday execution: time, SOPs, rules, seriousness, preparation, communication and professional conduct.

But discipline does not mean “we have always done it this way.”

When employees find a better, safer or more efficient way to work, they are expected to raise the idea through the proper process. The company should learn and improve rather than protect outdated methods.

## What it looks like — Discipline

- Be ready to work at the committed start time, not merely arrive at the premises.
- Inform the appropriate person as early as possible when lateness is unavoidable.
- Request leave through the proper process and provide a proper handover.
- Follow current rules even when you disagree with them.
- Maintain SOP discipline.
- Take work seriously.
- Maintain professional behaviour and communication.
- Do not use good technical performance as an excuse for repeated indiscipline.
- Treat company resources and materials responsibly.
- Do not use company resources for personal purposes without permission.

## What it looks like — Improvement

- Raise a practical problem instead of simply complaining about it.
- Suggest improvements with facts and a proposed solution.
- Share new techniques and useful knowledge with the team.
- Test and validate improvements before standardising them.
- Obtain approval before changing an established process.
- If a rule or SOP repeatedly creates a practical problem, raise the issue through the right channel.
- Learn new skills when they improve the team's capability.
- Support colleagues in learning.
- Treat mistakes and post-work observations as opportunities to improve the process.

## Improvement cycle

> **Observe → Suggest → Test → Validate → Approve → Standardise → Teach the team**

## What it does NOT mean

- It does **not** mean ignoring rules because you disagree with them.
- It does **not** mean changing an SOP informally.
- It does **not** mean implementing an untested shortcut.
- It does **not** mean speed at the expense of quality.
- It does **not** mean “continuous improvement” requires constant change for its own sake.
- It does **not** mean discipline is optional for high performers.

## Decision rule

> **Follow the current standard. If it can be better, improve it through evidence and the right approval process.**

## Interview scenario

> **“Tell me about a process you believed could be improved. What did you do instead of simply ignoring the existing process?”**

# 9. CLEAN TANK'S NON-NEGOTIABLE: QUALITY & SAFETY

Quality and Safety are deliberately treated as the **boundary around all five values**, rather than as another generic value.

This reflects the founder's repeated decision rule:

> **When workload, time, cost, customer pressure or profit conflicts with Quality or Safety, Quality and Safety do not get sacrificed.**

## Quality means

- Required standards are met consistently.
- Required process steps are not skipped for convenience.
- Customer concerns are heard, checked and addressed.
- The final site condition is part of the quality of the job.
- Handover is part of completion.
- “Acceptable” is not an excuse for careless execution when a better result is reasonably achievable.

## Safety means

- Unsafe work is not normalised because “nothing happened last time.”
- A safety concern can be raised regardless of hierarchy.
- If the work method creates a safety risk, stop and find a safe alternative.
- Required PPE must be available before work begins.
- A supervisor's urgency does not override a legitimate safety requirement.
- A safe method that requires additional time or cost should be used, with the impact communicated appropriately.

## The safety escalation rule

> **If a method may create a safety risk, do not proceed on assumption. Stop, raise the concern, and obtain a safe method or required approval.**

# 10. Integrity as an Operating Behaviour

Integrity is intentionally **not a separate headline value** in this version.

The founder's answers show that honesty and transparency are expected across every value.

Integrity therefore operates as a cross-cutting behaviour:

- Do not hide meaningful mistakes.
- Verify discrepancies before blaming someone.
- Report meaningful material or financial discrepancies.
- Do not take personal benefits from customers.
- Do not use company resources for personal purposes without permission.
- If a mistake can affect a customer, take responsibility for containing or correcting it and report it appropriately.
- Use facts rather than rumours or blame.
- If you do not have authority to promise something, say so and obtain the right confirmation.
- Protect company and customer interests even when doing so is personally uncomfortable.

### Core principle

> **Do the right thing even when hiding the problem would be easier.**

# 11. What These Values Mean in Real Situations

## Scenario 1 — Customer wants the job faster

**Situation:** The customer wants immediate completion, but speeding up would require skipping a required process step.

**Clean Tank response:**  
Complete the required process. Explain why the step matters. Do not trade process integrity for speed.

**Primary values:** Right Work, Right Way + Keep the Promise.

---

## Scenario 2 — Work is complete but the site is left messy

**Situation:** The main cleaning work is technically successful, but tools, waste or the work area have not been properly closed out.

**Clean Tank response:**  
The job is not complete. Finish the closure, restore the site and hand over properly.

**Primary value:** Right Work, Right Way.

---

## Scenario 3 — A teammate is struggling

**Situation:** Your own task is complete, but another worker is struggling to finish theirs.

**Clean Tank response:**  
Help them. If the issue requires authority or intervention, involve the right person.

**Primary values:** Own the Outcome + Respect People, Build the Team.

---

## Scenario 4 — Customer complaint after technically correct work

**Situation:** The customer believes part of the tank was not properly cleaned.

**Clean Tank response:**

1. Listen.
2. Go with the customer to inspect the concern.
3. Verify the facts.
4. If there is a genuine deficiency, correct it.
5. If the work meets the standard, explain calmly and show the basis for the conclusion.

**Primary values:** Right Work, Right Way + Keep the Promise.

---

## Scenario 5 — Customer asks for something outside scope

**Situation:** The customer requests additional work that was not agreed and the employee has no authority to approve it.

**Clean Tank response:**  
Do not promise it. Clarify the request, involve management and return with an authorised solution.

**Primary value:** Keep the Promise.

---

## Scenario 6 — Senior gives an unsafe instruction

**Situation:** A senior says, “Do it quickly; nothing will happen.”

**Clean Tank response:**  
Respectfully raise the safety concern and do not proceed without a safe method.

**Primary boundary:** Safety.

---

## Scenario 7 — Better process discovered

**Situation:** An employee finds a method that appears faster and equally or more effective.

**Clean Tank response:**

1. Share the idea.
2. Test/validate it.
3. Obtain approval.
4. Standardise it if proven.
5. Teach the team.

**Primary value:** Stay Disciplined, Keep Improving.

---

## Scenario 8 — Junior has a better idea

**Situation:** A junior employee knows a better method than a senior.

**Clean Tank response:**  
Listen, verify the facts, and use the better method if it is appropriate. Seniority does not make a person automatically right.

**Primary value:** Respect People, Build the Team.

---

## Scenario 9 — Employee makes a costly mistake

**Situation:** An employee makes a mistake that could create financial or customer impact.

**Clean Tank response:**  
First try to contain or correct the problem. Then report the meaningful issue appropriately so the company can manage the impact and prevent recurrence.

**Primary values:** Own the Outcome + Integrity.

---

## Scenario 10 — Workload becomes very high

**Situation:** There is limited manpower, limited time and heavy workload.

**Clean Tank response:**  
Re-plan, communicate, coordinate and prioritise — but do not reduce the required Quality or Safety standard simply to finish faster.

**Primary boundary:** Quality & Safety.

# 12. Hiring: How Clean Tank Should Test the Values

Values should not be judged by asking:

> “Are you hardworking?”

Most candidates will say yes.

Instead, interviewers should ask for **specific past behaviour**.

## Suggested interview questions

### Right Work, Right Way

> “Tell me about a time when you had to choose between doing something quickly and doing it properly. What did you do?”

Look for evidence of:
- standards;
- process discipline;
- judgement;
- safety awareness;
- willingness to correct work.

### Own the Outcome

> “Tell me about a time when the problem was not technically your responsibility, but you still helped solve it.”

Look for:
- initiative;
- problem solving;
- teamwork;
- sensible escalation;
- no blame mentality.

### Keep the Promise

> “Tell me about a commitment you made that became difficult to deliver. How did you handle it?”

Look for:
- realistic planning;
- early communication;
- accountability;
- authority awareness;
- customer responsibility.

### Respect People, Build the Team

> “Tell me about a time you had to correct someone less experienced than you.”

Look for:
- patience;
- dignity;
- teaching;
- private correction;
- accountability.

### Stay Disciplined, Keep Improving

> “Tell me about a rule or process you disagreed with. What did you do?”

Look for:
- discipline;
- respect for existing standards;
- constructive challenge;
- evidence;
- improvement mindset.

# 13. Performance Reviews: What Good Looks Like

Performance at Clean Tank should not be measured only by output.

A useful review should consider both:

**WHAT was delivered**  
and  
**HOW it was delivered.**

## Review prompts

### Right Work, Right Way
- Did the person consistently meet required standards?
- Did they follow required processes?
- Did they complete proper closure and handover?
- Did they protect quality under pressure?

### Own the Outcome
- Did they solve problems or merely report them?
- Did they help the team achieve the overall result?
- Did they escalate appropriately when authority was needed?

### Keep the Promise
- Did they honour commitments?
- Did they communicate risks early?
- Did they avoid unauthorised promises?

### Respect People, Build the Team
- Did they treat people with dignity?
- Did they help others learn?
- Did they accept better ideas from others?
- Did they correct respectfully?

### Stay Disciplined, Keep Improving
- Were they punctual and prepared?
- Did they follow current rules and SOPs?
- Did they suggest useful improvements?
- Did they learn and share new skills?

# 14. Recognition and Correction

## Recognise behaviours, not just results

Good performance should be openly appreciated when someone:

- protects quality despite pressure;
- stops unsafe work;
- takes ownership of a problem;
- helps a teammate succeed;
- admits and responsibly fixes a mistake;
- keeps an important customer commitment;
- improves a process through the proper channel;
- teaches another employee;
- respectfully challenges an incorrect or unsafe instruction.

## Correct behaviours, not dignity

When correction is needed:

1. Address the issue.
2. Explain the expected standard.
3. Listen to the employee's explanation.
4. Agree on the correction.
5. Observe whether the behaviour improves.
6. If repeated negligence continues after clear feedback, use appropriate disciplinary action.

### Clean Tank rule

> **Correct privately. Do not humiliate. Do not ignore repeated negligence.**

# 15. Culture Rituals That Reinforce the Values

The values should become visible through recurring habits.

## 1. Start-of-work readiness check

Before work begins:

- people ready;
- equipment ready;
- required PPE ready;
- scope understood;
- responsibilities clear.

**Reinforces:** Right Work, Right Way + Discipline.

## 2. End-of-job closure check

Before leaving a site:

- work checked;
- tools accounted for;
- waste handled correctly;
- site restored;
- customer handover completed;
- concerns recorded.

**Reinforces:** Right Work, Right Way + Own the Outcome.

## 3. Quick problem review

When something goes wrong, ask:

- What happened?
- What was the actual impact?
- What did we do to contain it?
- What should change so it does not repeat?

Do not begin with:

> “Whose fault was it?”

**Reinforces:** Own the Outcome + Improvement.

## 4. Knowledge sharing

When someone discovers a useful technique:

- share it;
- test it;
- validate it;
- standardise if appropriate;
- teach the team.

**Reinforces:** Respect People, Build the Team + Improvement.

## 5. Recognition habit

Regularly recognise specific behaviours, not vague statements such as “good job.”

Example:

> “You noticed the unsafe condition and stopped the work before anyone was exposed. That is how we work at Clean Tank.”

**Reinforces:** all values, especially Safety and Ownership.

# 16. The Clean Tank Decision Filter

When a difficult decision comes up, ask these questions in order:

### 1. Is it safe?

If not, stop and find a safe method.

### 2. Does it meet the required quality standard?

If not, correct it.

### 3. Are we owning the overall result?

If not, identify what still needs to happen.

### 4. Are we honouring the customer commitment?

If circumstances have changed, communicate through the right channel.

### 5. Are we treating people respectfully?

If not, change the way the issue is being handled.

### 6. Are we following the current standard?

If not, follow it unless an authorised change has been made.

### 7. Can the process be improved?

If yes, raise the improvement through the proper cycle.

# 19. Final Founder Principle

The strongest single idea to emerge from the founder interview is:

> **“I have done my work” is not the same as “the work is done.”**

At Clean Tank, responsibility does not end when an individual task is checked off.

The real finish line is:

**The work is right.  
The process was right.  
The work was safe.  
The customer received the right result.  
The team worked together.  
The site was properly handed over.  
And the company learned something that can make the next job better.**

That is what it means to **Own the Outcome**.
