For the owners

Clean Tank price brief

What we charge homes, what others in the Tricity and across India charge, and where we stand at each tank size. Plain words, real figures, and a clear line between what is decided and what is not.

In one minute

  • A normal 1,000 L home tank costs ₹800 with us before tax, or ₹944 with 18% GST. Urban Company charges ₹949. We are level.
  • Small underground tanks are where we are clearly cheaper: ₹1,180 for 2,000 L, against Urban Company's ₹1,699 plus helper charges.
  • Our weak spot is the 5,000 L overhead tank, at ₹1,652 with GST, about 47% above the typical price across India. We have decided to lower it. The new figure is not set.
  • Cheap local offers start at ₹399 to ₹500, usually with no tank size and less work. We will always be above them, and we should not chase them.
  • GST hurts us quietly. No rival says whether its price includes tax, and small operators may not charge it at all.
  • Nobody publishes a real Tricity price for societies or institutions. Government jobs go to the lowest bidder.
  • Nobody has phoned a competitor yet. Every rival figure here is a price shown online, not a price someone paid.

The rules around the list

  • GST at 18% goes on top. The rate is confirmed. The calculator on the website shows the price before tax first, then adds the tax.
  • More tanks in one visit cost less, for tanks up to 30,000 L: 2 or 3 tanks get 10% off, 4 or more get 20% off. Above 30,000 L the discount can be discussed.
  • The price drops at 30,000 L on purpose. Above that size the lower rate applies to the whole tank, so a 30,000 L tank costs ₹6,000 and a 30,001 L tank costs ₹3,900. The owner confirmed this is intended.
  • The price is the same everywhere we serve homes: the Tricity and up to 20 km around it.
  • A really hard-to-reach tank may cost a small extra. The customer is told before the visit. No rival publishes a figure for this either.
  • Housing societies, institutions, companies and government get no public price list. They get a quote, because the price depends on the work, the tank sizes, the place and the site.
  • AMC means annual maintenance contract: a yearly deal where we clean on a fixed schedule, remind the customer before each visit and keep the records. Our AMC pages can explain how it works, how often we visit and what paperwork comes with it, but the rupees stay on quote.
  • Franchise has no public price list either. The franchise investment figures in INFO-BANK are a separate matter and are not covered here.

2. Who we are compared with

About 70 local businesses clean tanks in the Tricity. Very few show a price. These are the ones that matter for price.

  • Urban Company. The national booking app. It sells tank cleaning in the Tricity at one fixed price in Chandigarh, Mohali, Panchkula, Zirakpur and Kharar. It only cleans tanks up to 6,000 L. Underground jobs cost extra if helpers are needed, at "market rates" with no figure. The ₹99 visit fee is waived on orders above ₹499.
  • Guga Aviation. Sector 37, Chandigarh. It has the fullest price card, and its price includes water testing and a certificate. It offers 20% off a first booking. Whether it really works in the Tricity is not proven.
  • CleanerKart. ₹699 a tank up to 1,000 L, in Chandigarh, Mohali, Panchkula, Zirakpur and Kalka. The customer supplies the disinfectant and the water, it does not serve Kharar, and it has no Google Maps listing. It is a smaller job than ours.
  • Cheap "from" prices. From ₹399 in a Facebook ad (500 to 2,000 L), from ₹499 at Grace and Fixomes, from ₹500 at ServiceSutra. None says what size the price is for, except the ad.
  • Typical price across India. The middle figure from about a dozen city and national price cards (Delhi, Gurugram, Bengaluru, Mumbai, Pune and others). Most of these firms do not work in the Tricity, so this is a yardstick only.
  • The local plumber or labourer. The rival our own product docs name first. No source gives a real Tricity figure for this work.

Welco, Shree Balaji, A-One and Wazara lead Google Maps here but publish no price. One Welco customer wrote that they paid a fixed ₹1,500 for an underground tank of unknown size and found it "a bit expensive". That is one review, so treat it as a hint.

Several tanks at once

Our 10% for 2 or 3 tanks is weaker than some rivals for two tanks of the same size, and better for an overhead tank plus a sump. Two 1,000 L tanks cost ₹1,699 with GST with us, against ₹1,099 to ₹1,299 at KaamGenie in Gurugram, which charges ₹400 to ₹600 for each extra tank. A 1,000 L overhead plus a 5,000 L sump costs ₹2,336 with us, inside KaamGenie Delhi's ₹2,200 to ₹3,000 for the same pair.

4. The cheapest offers and the plumber

We will always be dearer than these. The research advises against chasing them.

  • The cheapest public offers here are ₹399 (a Facebook ad, 500 to 2,000 L), ₹499 (Grace, Fixomes, homeserivces.in) and ₹500 (ServiceSutra). Nothing shows what the ₹399 includes.
  • A plumber or labourer probably charges ₹200 to ₹500 a tank, or ₹500 to ₹700 for a day's work. These figures come from other cities, a Mohali OLX ad at ₹500, and directory listings. No neutral Tricity source exists.
  • Against a ₹500 job, our ₹708 is about 1.4 times the price and our ₹944 about 1.9 times. Against a plumber taking half a day, our bigger sump prices are much closer.
  • Our own rules say we never criticise plumbers or labourers, and the website never names a rival or prints a price comparison.

The GST problem

A firm only has to register for GST above ₹20 lakh a year in sales. That is about 2,500 jobs at ₹800, or 7 a day, so a small one-van operator probably does not charge GST. A rival's ₹800 is then the final price, while ours becomes ₹944. No rival page in any round says whether its price includes GST. CleanerKart comes closest with "the price you book is the price you pay". The other side: one Tricity advertiser, Good Life Future, sells on giving a proper GST bill, so a real bill is a selling point for some buyers.

5. Societies, institutions and government

This is where the evidence is weakest. These buyers get private quotes, so almost nothing is public.

  • The only AMC prices published anywhere are KPS's yearly ranges: ₹6,000 to ₹15,000 for one building, ₹15,000 to ₹50,000 for a society, and ₹30,000 to ₹1,00,000 for commercial sites. The same figures sit on its Gurugram, Pune and Mumbai pages, so they are a template, not a local price. AI search tools still repeat them.
  • As a sum only: a society with two 1,00,000 L tanks cleaned twice a year would pay ₹52,000 before tax at our home rate of ₹0.13 a litre, or ₹61,360 with GST. That is just above KPS's society range.
  • Directory listings on IndiaMART show ₹0.20 to ₹0.60 a litre and ₹1 a litre for societies. Google's AI answers repeat the ₹0.20 to ₹0.60 range often. These are listings in mixed units and cannot be read as tank prices.
  • Other cities give society AMC discounts of 15% to 25% (Delhi) and 20% to 30% (Bengaluru).

7. How sure we are

All three research rounds ran on 31 October 2026. Nobody phoned or booked anything, so no price here was heard from a seller or paid by us.

  • Read live on the seller's own page. Urban Company (checked in five towns), Guga, CleanerKart, Grace, ServiceSutra, KPS. These are solid as listed prices.
  • Seen only in search results or directories. IndiaMART, Justdial and OLX figures such as ₹700 a day, ₹350, ₹0.60 a litre and the ₹500 OLX ad. Weak.
  • A firm's own "typical cost" guide. KaamGenie, KBS, ServiceSutra's guide, AR. Marketing ranges, not prices you can book.
  • Worked out or guessed. The typical-in-India figures (a middle value from thin samples, very thin above 2,000 L), the plumber's price, whether small firms charge GST, and the government figures for box-shaped tanks.

Where the sources disagree

The owner's own run from Kharar is the newest and the only one that read Google Search, Justdial and IndiaMART. Where it disagrees, trust it.

  • Fixomes "from ₹499": the first two rounds saw it; the Kharar run found no price in its copy of the page. Unresolved.
  • Sized price cards in the Tricity: the second round said only two (Urban Company and Guga). The Kharar run added CleanerKart for one size.
  • Urban Company's 2,000 L overhead and 5,000 L sump prices were unreadable in the first round. The second round read ₹1,149 and ₹1,899.
  • Safaiwale's ₹600, ₹700 and ₹800 were first reported as a Chandigarh list. They come from a template page for Vasundhara. Its Chandigarh page shows no price.
  • Favorz's ₹245 turned out to be a placeholder used on every service on its site. Dropped.
  • Mr Service Expert's ₹500 and ₹600 are on a national template. Not local evidence.
  • HomeTriangle's prices are hidden behind a city list with no Chandigarh in it. Used as a national yardstick only.
  • Guga's first-booking discount: one round worked it out on the 1,000 L price (about ₹1,199), the Kharar run on the 500 L price (₹799). Both are above our price for the same size.
  • Typical prices moved a little between rounds: 2,000 L overhead from ₹1,050 to ₹1,074, 5,000 L sump from ₹2,000 to ₹1,950. No verdict changed.
