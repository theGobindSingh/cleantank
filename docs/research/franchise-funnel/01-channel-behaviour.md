# Channel behaviour of Indian franchise prospects

Research for CT-443 (part of CT-442), 28 Nov 2026. It builds on the CT-409 notes in`docs/research/franchise/`, mainly`03-buyer-psychology.md` (WhatsApp first, family as second reader, KPMG-Google language data) and`05-market-demand-rules.md` (search terms, portals, YouTube, the DPDP checklist). Those points are not repeated here.

The question: James's starting thesis is email-first lead capture on`/franchise`. How do the people we want (small investors, the self-employed, job-leavers, family businesses, Tricity and tier-2/3 Punjab, Haryana and Himachal) actually use WhatsApp, email, calls, mobile, Hindi/Punjabi and search?

Strength: **strong** means a large government or probability sample, or direct measurement; **medium** means a large industry survey with a clear method, or a vendor dataset with a stated size; **weak** means vendor-commissioned without method, small, old, foreign, or seen only second hand.

## Summary

1. **There is no public data on how Indian franchise buyers choose a contact channel.** I found no survey from Franchise India, FAIFP, IFA, Francorp or any academic source that measures it. Every franchise-specific number below is American, old, or about what franchisors do. Any claim that "franchise buyers in India prefer email" (or WhatsApp) is a guess until we measure our own leads.
2. **Email is a minority skill in our region, even among internet users.** The government's CAMS survey (2022-23, 3 lakh households) found 38.4% of Punjab adults can send or receive email, against 67.2% who can use the internet and 59.7% who can send a message with an attachment (email, WhatsApp or SMS). Haryana is 37.8%, Chandigarh 39.8%, all-India 43.4%. Rural Punjab women: 29.9%. Our buyers have ₹5.5 lakh or more to invest and are likely more educated than average, so their email skill is higher than this. But their fathers and spouses, the second readers, are not.
3. **Our visitors will be on phones.** 84.2% of Indian web traffic was mobile in October 2026 (Statcounter), though the figure swings between about 65% and 84% month to month. Only 9.7% of Punjab households own a desktop or laptop (CAMS). Android is 94.8% of Indian mobiles.
4. **Mobile forms convert worse.** In Zuko's global dataset, enquiry (lead-gen) forms complete at 47.7% on desktop and 37.7% on mobile, the widest device gap of any form type. A form that asks for an email address on a phone adds one more field people have to type.
5. **The preference surveys contradict each other, which is the real finding.** A Meta-commissioned Kantar survey (2022) says over 70% of online Indians prefer messaging a business to email or calls. A Truecaller-commissioned Kantar survey (2026) says 76% prefer voice calls over email, SMS or chat. An undated Mobile Ecosystem Forum survey puts WhatsApp first (45%) with email close behind (41%), ahead of SMS (35%) and calls (33%). Each platform's own study favours that platform. Email does not come first in any of them.
6. **Cold calls are failing, and that hurts call-back promises too.** 95% of Indian mobile users get unwanted calls every day and 77% get three or more (LocalCircles, 2024, 71,000 responses). Businesses report that 79% of customers avoid calls from unknown numbers, and 49% of consumers say they would answer if they could schedule the call-back (Truecaller/Kantar 2026). A call-back from an unsaved number will often go unanswered.
7. **WhatsApp has a spam problem too.** 95% of WhatsApp users surveyed got unsolicited commercial messages every day, 41% got four or more, and "job and earning offers" were one of the main categories (LocalCircles, Feb 2023). An unrequested "earn with our franchise" WhatsApp sequence will read as that spam. WhatsApp works as a channel the prospect opens, not one we push.
8. **The biggest franchise portal treats the mobile number as the identity.** Franchise India's enquiry form asks for name, email, mobile, address, PIN code, state, city and investment range, but verifies only the mobile number, by OTP. Its newsletter box comes pre-ticked, which is not valid consent under DPDP.
9. **Email open rates in India are close to global ones, but opens are not replies.** GetResponse's 2024 benchmark puts India at 25.84% opens and 8.22% clicks (2023 data, its own customers). An insurance executive quoted in the Bain-Meta 2024 report says most business emails land in Promotions at "1%-2% open rates". No source I found measures email reply rates in India.
10. **Language: Hindi and Punjabi for reading and watching, English or Hinglish for typing and searching.** IAMAI-Kantar (2024, 91,000 face-to-face interviews) found 98% of Indian internet users use some Indic-language content and 57% of urban users prefer Indic content. But only 19% of Indic-language activity is search, against 81% for video. Punjabi is the mother tongue of 89.8% of Punjab (Census 2011). Chandigarh is 73% Hindi, 22% Punjabi. CT-409 found Punjab searchers type Hindi or English, not Gurmukhi.

## What this means for the /franchise funnel

These are proposals for CT-447 (pilot) and CT-448 (synthesis), not decisions.

- **Do not make email the required first step.** Ask for a mobile number as the one required contact field, with "Is this on WhatsApp?" as a tick. Offer email as an optional field for people who want the guide by email. This keeps the email path open for the minority who prefer it, without turning away the majority.
- **Let the prospect choose the channel and the time.** Three equal options after the guide or the readiness worksheet: "Send it on WhatsApp", "Call me at a time I pick" (slot picker, working hours), "Email it to me". Record the choice. The choice itself is the pilot's best data on preference, better than any survey above.
- **Make our calls recognisable.** Before any call, send a WhatsApp from the main number saying who will call and when. Call from that same number. Consider a verified business caller ID (Truecaller for Business or similar; cost and fit not checked). Never call before the slot the prospect picked.
- **Put the reply-time promise in writing and keep it.** HBR's audit (Oldroyd et al., 2011, in CT-409) found replies within an hour were about seven times more likely to qualify a lead. US franchisors manage a call-back only 44% of the time (Franchise Update Media, 2025). A same-working-day reply on WhatsApp is a real edge, if the owner can staff it (see CT-446).
- **No unrequested WhatsApp drip.** Anything after the first reply needs separate, unticked, easy-to-withdraw consent (DPDP; see`05-market-demand-rules.md`). Keep follow-ups few and useful: the guide, the worksheet result, a visit invitation.
- **Design for a phone first.** One column, large tap targets, the numeric keypad for the mobile field, no account creation, no OTP before we have given anything of value. Keep the first step to two or three fields; ask the qualifying questions after the prospect has the guide.
- **Make the guide easy to forward.** The second reader (father, spouse, brother) gets the link or PDF on WhatsApp. A plain page per language and a small PDF work better than an email attachment for that.
- **Hindi and Punjabi in full, with English kept close.** Forms accept Latin letters in every language, since few people type Gurmukhi. Consider a short founder video in Hindi/Punjabi (video is where Indic usage is highest). Search landing copy should match English and Hinglish queries.
- **Tag every lead by source, locale, device and channel chosen** (UTM, page language, user agent class), with no personal data in analytics. That gives us the franchise-specific evidence that does not exist publicly.

## Evidence that supports email (so it is not dismissed)

- MEF's survey puts email second (41%), only four points behind WhatsApp (45%), and ahead of calls. Date and sample unknown.
- Our buyers are a selected group: people with ₹5.5 lakh to ₹20 lakh to invest, often ex-employees or business owners. Their email use is likely well above the CAMS population figure. Ex-corporate job-leavers in Mohali or Chandigarh may well prefer email.
- Bain-Meta (2024) found financial firms still prefer email for sensitive documents. A franchise agreement draft and GST invoices may fit email better than WhatsApp.
- Every franchise portal and most franchisor forms we looked at (CT-409,`02-adjacent-best-in-class.md`) collect email. It is the norm, even if it is not the verified field.
- Email costs nothing per message, while WhatsApp Business API messages are charged per message by Meta.

None of this makes email the best *first* step. It does argue for keeping email as one option and for using it where documents matter.

## Open questions

1. **How did past franchise enquiries reach the owner?** Phone, WhatsApp, email, IndiaMART, Justdial, word of mouth? Even a rough count from the last two years would be the only franchise-specific evidence for our own region. (Asked on CT-443.)
2. Did past franchise prospects ever send or ask for things by email (brochure, agreement)? Which ones?
3. Who answers the franchise WhatsApp and calls today, and in what hours? This sets the reply-time promise (CT-446).
4. Is a verified business caller ID worth the cost? Not checked.
5. What does WhatsApp Business API cost per message for us, if we automate guide delivery? Meta's India rate card changes; check at build time.
6. How many prospects share a phone with family (IAMAI: 18% of users go online on someone else's phone)? It matters for OTP and for who reads our messages. The pilot can only see this indirectly.
7. Franchise India may share anonymised lead data (device, channel, city tier) with listed brands. Worth asking if we list there.
