# Paper and restraint make cards look expensive

A premium visiting card gets its effect from thick, uncoated, deliberately chosen stock, one or two solid inks, a pressed impression, and very little information. Foil and film matter far less. Clean Tank Services should run two cards. Founders and senior people get a bonded card of about 800 to 1,000 µm, white cotton on the information side and dyed navy on the identity side, letterpressed in navy with one coral painted edge. Staff get a 350 to 400 gsm white uncoated card with names printed digitally, because craft processes need a plate or die per name and 5 to 10 people at 100 cards each makes that impractical. The hardest technical problem is the navy: #20334B is darker than any standard uncoated Pantone reproduces on screen values, so navy on uncoated paper needs a custom-mixed ink, a double hit or a dyed sheet. The brief's "no gloss" rule removes spot UV, thermography, metallic foil and gilding, which covers most of what Indian online printers sell as premium. India can make the single-ply cotton card today through at least one confirmed Delhi studio, while the navy duplex needs phone calls to paper merchants and printers before anyone commits to it. Nearly all the evidence here comes from printers and merchants. No perception study was found and no Indian printer was contacted, so physical swatches and a proof must settle what this report cannot.

## Caliper and fibre separate a premium card from a heavy one

Premium cards come from three material families: true cotton (Crane Lettra, Conqueror Connoisseur, Gmund Cotton), dyed-through virgin pulp (Colorplan, Keaykolour), and coated board under soft-touch film. Each buys something different. Cotton buys softness and bulk: Lettra is **100% cotton and uncalendared**, which leaves loose fibres and texture that take a deep impression ([Legion Paper](https://www.legionpaper.com/cranes-lettra); [Boxcar Press](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=6832)). Dyed pulp buys colour: Colorplan is ECF virgin fibre in about **55 colours**, listed for letterpress, offset, silkscreen and foil ([Legion Paper](https://legionpaper.com/colorplan)). Soft-touch buys a velvet first touch, but the hand is on polypropylene film ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md)), so the paper underneath stops mattering.

Grams per square metre mislead across these families. From listed figures, Lettra at 595 gsm calipers about 1,024 µm, a bulk of 1.72 cm³/g, while Colorplan at 702 gsm calipers 965 µm, or 1.37 cm³/g ([Paper Mill Store](https://www.thepapermillstore.com/crane-s-lettra-pearl-white-paper-8-1-2-x-11-in-220-lb-cover-dt-lettra-letterpress-100-cotton-50-per-package.html); [Legion Paper](https://legionpaper.com/colorplan)). **A cotton card is thicker at a lower gsm than a dyed one, so Clean Tank should specify caliper in microns and treat gsm as secondary.** The Lettra caliper's unit is assumed to be points, since the listing omits it, and no mill data sheet could be opened for most of these grades.

Printer language puts **350 gsm as the professional default and 400 gsm as "a small upgrade that gets noticed"** ([Xcel UK](https://xceluk.com/business-card-paper-weights-finishes/)). Duplex at 600 to 700 gsm "does not flex", and triplex at 900 to 1,000 gsm has tile-like rigidity but can read as novelty ([Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)). This research reads those tiers for India as: 300 to 350 gsm commodity, 400 ordinary premium, 540 to 700 dyed or 600 cotton real premium, and 800 and up statement only. Extra thickness probably stops helping at about 1 mm, once the card no longer flexes at all. That threshold is reasoning, and the tier language throughout is marketing with no perception research behind it. There is a practical limit too: a 20-card holder takes only 10 to 12 duplex cards ([Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)), which is one reason thickness belongs on cards handed out selectively.

## Most thick cards are two or three sheets glued together

Duplexing is a lamination process with a fixed order. Each sheet prints flat, adhesive goes on one face, a mounting press joins the sheets and pushes out air, the bond cures, and only then is the card trimmed, so misaligned halves leave no lip. **Cold glue on a roller is the usual bond**, with heat-activated film for laminated stock that glue struggles to wet ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them)). One UK studio allows up to 7 days for a full cure ([After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/us/faqs/production/thickest-business-card)), and another US printer adds a day or two to production ([Dolce Press](https://dolcepress.com/finishing/duplexing)). Each side can be a different colour or stock ([Dolce Press](https://dolcepress.com/finishing/duplexing)), and that is what makes a white-and-navy card possible.

The failure modes matter in India. Adhesive can creep in heat, uncoated fibres absorb humidity and can lift a corner, and a scored duplex cracks along the glue line ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them)). Monsoon and summer conditions make the first two real risks, so a test batch should sit through both a hot car and a humid week before the full run. No Indian source describes adhesives or cure times, and no delamination data exists. Folded designs are out.

A triplex adds a coloured core, commonly 250 gsm silk, 300 gsm colour and 250 gsm silk ([After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/portfolio/triple-thick-800gsm-gold-foil-business-card/)). The core shows mainly edge-on, as "a tactile surprise", with two bond lines instead of one ([Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)). A coral core between navy faces would be the strongest brand match available, and the least obtainable: it needs a coral dyed sheet, doubles the bond risk, and no Indian vendor in this research confirmed even a plain duplex. The term "multiloft" appeared in no source as a defined process, so it should not go on a specification.

Painted edges are the practical substitute, and they set a hard thickness floor. **32 pt, about 813 µm, is the practical minimum**: 14 pt barely shows an edge and 38 to 48 pt gives a wider band ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/guide/how-to-make-painted-edge-business-cards)). That rules out Colorplan 540 gsm at 711 µm and admits 700 gsm or 600 gsm cotton. Paint needs two thin coats on a clamped stack, and failures include paint seeping onto faces and uncoated fibres soaking up colour (same source). Faces need a 1/8 inch (3.18 mm) foil-free margin when edge work is planned ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963)). 4OVER4 contradicts itself on whether paint works over laminate ([duplex guide](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them); [soft-touch guide](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md)), so stay on uncoated stock. One US vendor sets a **250-card minimum per design** because a stacked batch is painted at once ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/help/what-is-the-minimum-quantity-for-edge-painted-business-cards.md)). A 100-card founder run may therefore need a printer willing to paint a smaller stack, or a stack of mixed names cut to the same size.

Square corners suit this brand. No source shows that either shape reads as more premium, but [docs/DESIGN.md](../../../DESIGN.md) caps radii at 4 px and describes a ruled spec sheet, and rounding is an unnecessary die step on thick board. If rounding is ever wanted, 1/8 inch (3.18 mm) is a common standard ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/help/what-is-the-difference-between-3-8-1-4-and-1-8-rounded-corners.md)) and the printer must round before painting.

## Navy is the decision that drives the rest of the card

Three routes reach a navy surface, and each fails differently. Heavy dark flood running to the trim is linked to edge cracking, because extra ink saturates fibres and makes coated surfaces brittle where cut ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/marketing-materials/why-does-my-business-card-crack-around-the-edges)), and lamination is the usual protection ([Primoprint](https://support.primoprint.com/articles/1427030-cracking)). Film, in turn, burnishes. Sources disagree here: a vendor says soft-touch deepens navy and resists fingerprints and scuffs ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md)), while a book-production reference says burnishing makes irreversible glossy patches at corners and edges, fingerprints leave dark or shiny marks, and **large dark solids show all of it most** ([MyBookQuality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/materials/anti-scuff-soft-touch-finishes.html)). A second printer also rates soft-touch slightly more prone to fingerprints and scuffing than gloss or matte ([Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/soft-touch-lamination/)). The analogy from book covers is imperfect, but the mechanism fits a card that lives in pockets. A shiny worn corner would also put gloss back on a no-gloss card.

**Dyed-through stock is the better navy surface**, because colour runs through the sheet and no white core appears at a cut or a chip. This conclusion rests on mechanism, since no controlled scuff test compares dyed stock with flood print. It carries two costs. Dark paper takes no dark type, and letterpress ink is translucent, so light ink on dark stock shows the paper ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/)). Type on that side must be foil, which is far more opaque ([Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/foil-stamping/)), or blind. And the colour is whatever the mill made: no Lab or hex value could be found for Colorplan Imperial Blue, Keaykolour Navy Blue or Gmund Gentlemen Blue, so the match to #20334B is unverified until a swatch sits beside a reference. Both costs push toward the same build, a dark sheet carrying only the identity bonded to a light sheet carrying the variable information. Boxcar recommends exactly this route, duplexing dark paper to light ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/)).

Spot inks are the right way to print both brand colours. Screen-value matching of the brand hexes against one Pantone dataset, using ΔE2000 ([coated](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brettapeters/pantones/master/pantone-coated.json); [uncoated](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/brettapeters/pantones/master/pantone-uncoated.json)), gave these nearest matches:

| Target | Coated candidates (ΔE00) | Uncoated candidates (ΔE00) |
|---|---|---|
| Navy #20334B, Lab 20.8 / 0.4 / -17.2 | 533 C 4.58; 534 C 4.53; 7546 C 4.89 (greyer) | 282 U 9.84; 289 U 9.87 |
| Coral #EE7555, Lab 63.0 / 44.2 / 39.2 | 7416 C 3.99 | 7579 U 2.86; 1655 U 3.03 |

The coral is easy on either surface. The navy is not: **the uncoated simulations sit near L30 to L35 against the target's L21**, so a standard U ink reads as faded slate-indigo. Hence a custom-mixed darker ink, a double hit, or a dyed sheet. These are simulations from an undated dataset with no 2000-series colours, and Pantone itself warns that on-screen values may not match its standards ([Pantone](https://www.pantone.com/color-finder/7416-c)). A physical drawdown on the chosen stock outranks every number in the table. Choose by the stock, too: same-number C and U swatches differ noticeably, and 7416 U is reported as pinker than the brand coral, which is why 7579 U leads the uncoated column.

CMYK is the fallback. Formula conversions give #EE7555 as C0 M51 Y64 K7 ([Brandkit](https://brandkit-4m0.pages.dev/color/EE7555)) and #20334B as about C57 M32 Y0 K71, but neither is a press value, and no ICC conversion was possible here. A screened CMYK navy on uncoated stock will look mottled and lose edge definition around reversed type, and a misregistered multi-ink edge blurs small text ([MyBookQuality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/printing/color-to-color-misregister.html)). If CMYK is forced, the printer should convert with its own profile and stay at or under the 300% ink limit of the uncoated Fogra52 profile ([ICC registry](https://registry.color.org/profile-registry/PSOuncoated_v3_FOGRA52)). Nobody knows yet whether Indian shops calibrate to Fogra standards.

Coral can reach the card three ways, and it should use only one per card. Ink is simplest. Paint needs a custom mix, since one vendor's palette ran to 16 colours ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them)). Foil is the weakest match: pigment foil gives dense opaque colour in matt, with more than 40 shades from Foilco ([People of Print](https://peopleofprint.com/product/you-dont-have-to-shine-to-stand-out-foilco-on-the-art-of-pigment-foil/)), and Crown Roll Leaf can match to Pantone ([Crown Roll Leaf](https://crownrollleaf.com/color-matching/)), but no coral stock foil was found, Indian supply is unknown, and foil beside ink shows the difference. Coral is also a marker only. By calculation and per DESIGN.md, it reaches **2.87:1 on white and 4.47:1 on navy, against 12.83:1 for navy on white**, so it carries no small type and no QR code.

## Only a few finishes survive a no-gloss brief

Published minimums conflict because they are house rules. Foil line minimums run from 0.25 pt at [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963) to 1 pt at [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines) and 2 pt at [Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork). The right response is to design to the cautious end of each range, since Indian printers publish no figures of their own in the sources reached.

| Finish | How it works | Design floor for this card | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Letterpress | Polymer plate presses ink into soft stock; no tints | 8 pt type ([Radix](https://printing.radix.coop/page/letterpress-file-guidelines)); 0.5 pt lines as a margin over the 0.25 pt published floor; reversed 12 pt ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/)) | Use on cotton; solids over 1/2 inch show a suede texture |
| Blind deboss | Die presses with no ink or foil | 0.6 mm (1.7 pt) line, 0.2 to 0.8 mm depth ([XC Paperbox](https://xcpaperbox-com-2ws.pages.dev/finishes/debossing/)) | Use for the CT mark; vector art only |
| Matte pigment foil | Heated brass die transfers opaque film, 120 to 180 °C ([Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/)) | 1 pt lines ([MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines)); 8 pt type, reversed 10 pt ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963)) | Use on navy if white or coral matte foil is obtainable |
| Painted edge | Paint on a clamped stack after trim | 32 pt card, 3.18 mm clear margin | Use for the coral |
| Soft-touch film | BOPP film by heat and pressure | Proof dark colours | Fallback only; poor on navy flood |
| Metallic foil, gilding | Reflective film | Not applicable | Out, as gloss by appearance |
| Spot UV | Clear raised coating | Not applicable | Out; also risks QR scanning |
| Thermography | Resin powder melted into a raised glossy layer ([4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-is-thermography-printing-and-how-raised-ink-works)) | Not applicable | Out; shiny, and read as imitation engraving |

Heat can scorch dark stocks under a deboss die, and a thin stock shows deboss on its reverse ([Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/); [XC Paperbox](https://xcpaperbox-com-2ws.pages.dev/finishes/debossing/)). Duplexing solves show-through if the impression happens before bonding, though no source documents that order for duplex work, so it needs confirming with the printer. Heavily textured stock gives patchy foil adhesion and thin-stroked serifs break down ([Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/)). Boxcar also warns against tight tracking and against heavy coverage mixed with fine detail in one foil colour ([Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963)). Only "embossing last" is directly sourced ([Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/)); the fuller sequence of print, foil, deboss, bond and cure, trim, then paint is assembled from the sources.

**Registration tolerance should shape the layout.** Foil to print is quoted at ±0.5 mm ([Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/)), up to 1/16 inch or 1.59 mm ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/faq/what-should-i-know-about-foil-stamping-and-registration-accuracy.md)), and about 3 mm ([Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork)). Trim drifts up to 1 mm ([Onlineprinters](https://onlineprinters.ie/c/faq/cutting-tolerances)). A 1 pt line is 0.35 mm, thinner than the best tolerance, so any foil or deboss tracing a printed shape will show its error. Each process should stand alone, with no foil keylines, no deboss framing print, and no front-to-back alignment. Thin borders echoing the trim are also unsafe: a border 3 mm in, shifted 1 mm, reads as 2 mm on one side and 4 mm on the other. The site's full-width ruled look survives this if rules are horizontal and stop short of the edges at the margin.

## Spec-sheet restraint does the visual work

No study or measured celebrated card gives a name-to-details ratio, and no designer on record explains why small type reads as expensive. Print guidance still converges. [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work) gives a name of 10 to 12 pt, role and contact of 7 to 8 pt, a 7 pt floor for anything that must be read, four levels at most, one alignment edge, and levels separated by "size and space first, weight second, color last", with one accent applied once. Its best line is that "11 next to 10 reads as a mistake, 11 next to 8 reads as a decision". [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477) agrees on sizes and adds compensation for the materials here: **1 to 1.5 pt more for reversed type and 0.5 to 1 pt more on cotton**, no Light weights below 9 pt, and no condensed faces at 7 pt and below. Working those together gives a name-to-contact ratio of about 1.5:1, which is derived from vendor advice and is not an observed property of acclaimed cards.

The brand already has most of the answer. Anek for name and values, IBM Plex Mono caps for labels at +0.08em to +0.10em, within [Butterick's](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html) 5 to 12% range for caps, and text labels such as TEL, MOB and MAIL in place of icons. The icon evidence is weak on both sides, but a labelled field looks like a record and needs no pictogram. Mono should stay on labels and short codes, because whole contact blocks in mono read slowly. Two labelled columns split at about one third, one or two ruled groups, and baseline steps of about 3.5 mm give the spec-sheet effect with no ornament. Too many fields turn the card into a form, so it should imply a record rather than simulate one. The nearest documented precedent is Bunch's identity for structural engineers Nosive Strukture, a "stark, technical identity" on a triplex card where structure and stock carry the feel ([BP&O](https://bpando.org/2012/12/13/branding-nosive-strukture)). Build's stationery for Generation Press used blue card edges and blind emboss in the same stripped-back way ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/build-generation-press)). No laboratory or instrument-maker card with designer commentary turned up.

The research notes proposed 0.25 pt hairlines. That is the offset positive floor ([Jukebox](https://support.jukeboxprint.com/en/articles/4214205-what-is-the-smallest-font-size-i-should-use)), and fine strokes break up on textured stock ([Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477)), so 0.5 pt is the sensible rule weight here.

The mark is settled by the repo. The card uses the outlined lockup in DESIGN.md, with the CT monogram for tight or square uses. The older droplet-and-swoosh logo in INFO-BANK breaks the no-gloss and no-gradient rules. The lockup's descriptor line is the size constraint: it sits at 12.82 units on a 236-unit width, so **the lockup must be at least 45.5 mm wide for a 7 pt descriptor, and 65 mm wide to reach the 10 pt reversed foil floor**. At 89 mm trim that leaves it fitting, but the identity side has room for little else. Blind deboss cannot hold that descriptor at a 1.7 pt line, so a blind treatment should use the monogram.

Content follows the same discipline: name, designation, mobile, email, web, one office, and one quiet ISO line. ISO's own guidance excludes the ISO logo ([ISO](https://help.iso.org/en/articles/375997-can-i-use-the-iso-logo)), so ISO stays as text, using the owner-approved wording in INFO-BANK and never "accredited". Hold the GSTIN off the card for now: the research flags`09LFS1473F5ZU` as 13 characters and INFO-BANK calls it correct, so the owner must check it, and nothing found requires a GSTIN on a visiting card anyway ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/display-gst-registration-certificate-gstin)). Add a small company line on the information side as well, so that a photographed or scanned card still says who it came from. QR codes that repeat printed data are a named fault ([Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)) and the default card should skip one. If one is wanted, print it flat in navy on white, about 20 mm plus a four-module quiet zone ([Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/)), which for a short URL at about Version 3 comes to about 25.5 mm of the live height. Foil and raised QR codes do not scan reliably ([4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/finish/can-i-print-a-qr-code-with-metallic-or-raised-foil-stamping)). NFC is a PVC or metal card of about 0.76 to 0.88 mm at 86 x 54 mm ([RFIDSolution](https://rfidsolution.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NFC-Business-Card-NXP-NTAG-213-86-54-0.88-mm-datasheet-RS-N213-CP-8654.pdf)), a separate object rather than a paper add-on.

Indian practice supports a card treated with some ceremony. Cards are offered with both hands or the right hand and read before being put away ([Cultural Atlas](https://theculturalatlas.org/indian-culture/indian-culture-business-culture)), and an Indian HR report calls them "deeply cultural" ([People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp/article/business/qr-code-or-classic-card-how-are-you-swapping-contacts-this-year-45770)). Workplaces are hierarchical, so the designation must be visible. Sources conflict on whether Hindi is needed, and they are mostly written for foreign visitors. "S." and "Sardar" are real forms before a Sikh name ([Onomastikon](https://tekeli.li/onomastikon/India/Sikh/Names.html)), though SGPC records an official preference for Shri ([SGPC](https://sgpc.net/sardar/)), and the owner's approved form on file is "Mr." Printing the bare name is the quietest course, and the choice is his. No Indian or water-services card of note turned up, though the Behance and Kyoorius archives could not be searched.

## India can make the white card today; the navy duplex needs phone calls

Online vendors cover the commodity end and stop there. Vistaprint India sells 350 gsm Velvet Touch ([Vistaprint India](https://www.vistaprint.in/business-cards/velvet-touch-visiting-cards)) and a range that includes Spot UV and Raised Foil ([Vistaprint India](https://www.vistaprint.in/business-cards)). Printo sells velvet cards on 300 or 350 gsm coated paper with spot UV or foil, foil in gold or silver only, one side only, minimum 100 ([Printo](https://printo.in/categories/premiumfinish-business-cards/customizable-products/velvet-touch-business-cards)). PrintStop's blog claims 300 to 400+ gsm with PVC, linen and eco stocks ([PrintStop](https://www.printstop.co.in/blog/best-visiting-card-printing-companies-india)), though that is its own marketing. **No mainstream online vendor confirmed 600 gsm cotton or duplex coloured-core cards.** Soft-touch at 350 gsm, in other words, is India's ordinary online premium product, so it will not set Clean Tank apart. Printland, Inkmonk, Zoomin, Printasia and MOO's delivery to India stayed unconfirmed.

Specialist capacity is thin and lightly verified. **Gravitfy Studio in Delhi is the one confirmed lead: letterpress on 400 to 600 gsm cotton, minimum 100, 10 to 14 working days, pan-India delivery** ([Gravitfy Studio](https://gravitfystudio.com/product/letterpress-printing-gravitfy-studio-delhi/)). Its page has oddities, including share links for an unrelated product, so confirm by phone. Chase the Key in Bangalore ([BIC](https://bangaloreinternationalcentre.org/?p=58486)) and Famous Letterpress in Dimapur ([Morung Express](https://morungexpress.com/fusion-store-welcomes-3-aesthetic-brands)) are real studios, and Invitations Design Studio's Fine Letter Press took outside work in dated coverage ([PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/invitations-design-studio-adopt-innovative-approach-wedding-cards-printing-24541)). Current card orders were not confirmed at any of them. A short search found no trace of The Letterpress Studio, Letterpress Delhi, Blue Ink, Paper Love, The Press Room, Baker Street Press or Haiku Press. Letterpress in India mostly serves security and economical short-run work ([Labels & Labeling](https://www.labelsandlabeling.com/features/letterpress-india)), and fine foil and letterpress craft appears to live in wedding stationery. Chawri Bazaar's Gaurav Cards lists gold foil and emboss ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.in/wedding-invitations/gaurav-cards-chawri-bazar--e6519)), and in Chandigarh, Arjana Printers ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.in/wedding-invitations/arjana-printers--e141723)) and Taaz Creative Solutions, with foil on art paper ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/taazcreativesolutions/printing-service.html)), are starting points. Nothing was found in Kharar or for card work in Mohali. Whether any of these shops will take a visiting-card job, or can bond heavy board, is a phone question.

Paper supply follows a similar pattern. Sona Papers in Delhi announced Conqueror, Curious and Rives in 2017 ([PrintWeek India](https://www.printweek.in/news/sona-papers-announces-partnership-arjowiggins-23876)) and said in 2019 it would add Keaykolour ([PrintWeek India](https://printweek.in/news/sona-to-bring-arjowiggins-keaykolour-range-of-specialty-papers-to-india-41341?amp=1)). Antalis took those brands in 2022 and moved manufacture ([PrintWeek](https://printweek.com/articles/antalis-buys-arjo-brands)), so current supply needs confirming. Albero Papers reportedly brought Sona to Chandigarh ([Print-Publishing](https://www.print-publishing.com/?p=6984)), which would be the natural first call from Kharar, though only a headline supports it. Gmund planned an Indian launch for January 2025 through Mumbai's Srinivas Paper and Jay Raj Fine Paper ([PrintWeek India](https://printweek.in/news/drupa-2024-gmund-papers-to-enter-the-indian-market-early-next-year-58931)). **Colorplan has no confirmed Indian stockist**, and "Sundeep Papers" could not be found at all. In practice, navy sheets seem likely to arrive at 250 to 300 gsm, not 700, so the bonded build is the realistic route and "600 gsm" quoted in India may itself mean two 300 gsm sheets. Ask what the stock is.

Staff runs are probably feasible, with one catch. A 100-card minimum at Printo and Gravitfy matches a 100-per-person order, and Vistaprint India accepts uploaded designs at that quantity ([Vistaprint India](https://www.vistaprint.in/business-cards/velvet-touch-visiting-cards)). A brass die lasts longer than magnesium and can be reused ([VSP Creative](https://vsp-creative.squarespace.com/which-die-do-you-need)), and Indian suppliers list magnesium blocks and brass dies ([A1 Blocks](https://m.indiamart.com/a1-blocks)), so a logo block can carry over to new staff if the printer will store it. Who keeps blocks is undocumented. The catch is order: printing names digitally after pressing the logo means putting finished stock through a digital press, which heavy cotton or duplex board may not survive. That reasoning is untested, and no Indian printer publishes such a workflow.

## The recommended specification

| Item | Founder and senior card | Staff card |
|---|---|---|
| Trim and bleed | 89 x 51 mm, the size confirmed by [Vistaprint India](https://www.vistaprint.in/visiting-cards/rounded-corner); 3 mm bleed, 95 x 57 mm file; 5 mm margin, 79 x 41 mm live area | Same |
| Corners | Square | Square |
| Build | Duplex, about 300 gsm white cotton plus about 300 gsm dyed navy; 813 µm minimum, 1,000 µm maximum. Fallback: single-ply 600 gsm white cotton | Single-ply white uncoated, 350 to 400 gsm |
| Information side | Letterpress, custom-mixed navy drawn down against #20334B | Navy ink; names digital, or offset spot navy shells if the printer can run them |
| Identity side | Dyed navy with lockup in white matte foil at 65 mm or wider; fallback blind deboss of the CT mark | Navy lockup on white, 45.5 mm or wider |
| Coral | Painted edge, one use only, custom mixed | One short Level Line in spot coral (7579 U starting point) |
| Type | Name 12 pt, contact 8.5 pt, labels and ISO line 8 pt | Name 11 pt, contact 7.5 to 8 pt, labels and ISO line 7 pt |
| Rules | 0.5 pt navy, horizontal only | Same |

The founder's type sizes add cotton compensation to the vendor values and respect the 8 pt letterpress floor. The staff card has no navy flood on purpose: a flood at 350 gsm forces either the weak uncoated navy or a film, and that card gets handled most. If cards are needed before any of this is sorted, a 350 gsm matte-laminated card from Printo or Vistaprint India, with no foil and no spot UV, is the stopgap, proofed for navy.

Supply files as vector PDF/X-4 with outlined fonts and 300 dpi images, since PDF/X-4 retains spot colours ([Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/what-is-a-pdf-x1a-2001)). Indian guidance agrees on outlines, 300 dpi and 3 mm bleed ([Printster](https://printster.in/print/blog/how-to-print-visiting-cards-online)). Printers differ on foil setup, between separate pages and black-and-white finish files ([Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork); [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines)), so send both a composite with named 100% spots such as`FOIL_WHITE` and`DEBOSS`, and one black vector PDF per finish, plus a mockup showing placement. Check overprint in preflight, because spots can change behaviour on export ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/indesign-discussions/help-preparing-document-with-spot-colors-for-printing-on-heidelberg-versafire/m-p/12748419/highlight/true)).

Six decisions belong to the owner: whether to use an honorific, which office and number the card carries, whether the ISO line prints "valid to 31 March 2029", which would date the card, whether a separate Hindi or Punjabi run is wanted, whether the GSTIN appears once checked, and whether any people other than founders get the bonded card. English alone keeps the navy side empty, so a smaller Indic run for government and field use is cleaner than three languages on one card. That run uses Anek Devanagari or Gurmukhi sized up, with no caps or letter-spacing, and Plex Mono labels fall back to Anek, per DESIGN.md.

## Conclusion

The no-gloss rule does more than remove finishes. It moves Clean Tank away from what the Indian online market calls premium and toward a small group of paper merchants and wedding-stationery craftspeople, where the card's quality depends on a person-to-person specification. It also exposes a conflict inside the brief: the processes that feel most expensive fix their content to a plate, while a staff card changes with each hire. Splitting the system into a bonded card with few names and a well-set uncoated card with many resolves that, and the two stay one family through type, rules and colour.

The work now is physical. Before any file is finalised, get Keaykolour and Gmund navy swatches beside a #20334B reference, one custom ink drawdown on cotton, and a bonded test card left through heat and humidity. If no dyed sheet comes close to #20334B, the fallback is single-ply white cotton with a coral edge, which should clear the 32 pt floor and loses only the navy face. That is the outcome to plan around, since Gravitfy's listing covers all of it except the coral paint.
