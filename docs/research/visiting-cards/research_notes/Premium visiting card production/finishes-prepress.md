# Print finishing techniques and prepress specifications for a premium business card

Target: Clean Tank Services card, steel-navy #20334B and coral #EE7555, no gloss, no gradients, printed in India. Researched 2026-11-01.

Evidence tags: findings marked [snippet] come from search-result summaries where the page itself was not opened, so the attribution to that exact URL is less certain. Everything else was read from the fetched page. Almost all sources are vendor pages, so numbers are house rules, not standards. Conversions used throughout: 1 pt = 0.353 mm, 0.25 pt = 0.088 mm, 0.5 pt = 0.176 mm, 1/16 in = 1.59 mm, 1/8 in = 3.18 mm.

## 1. How each finish works, what it needs, and how it fails

### Takeaway
Matte-compatible finishes that suit the brief are letterpress or blind deboss on thick cotton, matte pigment foil, and painted edges. Spot UV, thermography and metallic foil or gilding all add shine, so they conflict with "no gloss". Design every finish to about 1 pt or heavier and never align it tightly to print: published minimums go as low as 0.25 pt, but those are best cases.

### Cited Findings

**Letterpress**
- Polymer plate presses ink into the sheet. Boxcar minimums: 0.25 pt (0.003 in) lines, "no hairlines", 6 pt type — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/). Radix is stricter at 8 pt type, with 0.25 pt lines and 1 pt dots on polymer — [Radix](https://printing.radix.coop/page/letterpress-file-guidelines).
- A newer Boxcar post gives plate-specific limits: KF152 plate 0.35 pt lines and 1 to 1.25 pt dots — [Boxcar blog](https://www.boxcarpress.com/blog/tag/letterpress-file-prep-tips/) [snippet].
- Halftones and tints are not recommended; use a second lighter ink. Solids over 1/2 in show paper through with a suede-like texture, and large solids can buckle thin stock — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/).
- Letterpress inks are translucent, so light ink on dark paper shows the paper. White or metallic can be run twice at extra cost. Boxcar's route to dark colour is an offset flood on the back of light paper, or duplexing dark paper to light — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/).
- Thin text in the same colour as a large solid needs a separate press run for a good impression — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/).
- 100% cotton is called the gold standard for letterpress, and an Indian supplier describes 600 gsm cotton as thick, uncoated and suited to letterpress and foil — [SA Creative](https://sacreativestore.lovable.app/visiting-card-printing) [snippet].

**Hot foil stamping**
- A custom metal die, typically brass, is heated to 120 to 180 °C on a heated plate. Multi-colour foil needs a separate die and pass per colour — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/).
- Line minimums conflict widely: 0.25 pt — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963); 0.5 pt — [Aura Print](https://aura-print.com/usa/foil-printing-problems) [snippet]; 0.75 (units not stated in the summary) — [Minted](https://minteddesign.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/6613472346125) [snippet]; 0.3 mm (0.85 pt) — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/); 0.8 mm — [Printswell](https://www.printswell.com/hubfs/Foil%20Setup%20Guide%20copy%201.pdf?hsLang=en) [snippet]; 1 pt — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/help/artwork-and-design/line-art-and-text-recommendations-for-spot-uv-and-foil) and [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines); 2 pt, with thinner lines refused — [Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork).
- Type minimums: 8 pt for most fonts, reversed 10 pt — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963); 7.5 pt sans on coated paper, 10 pt+ serif — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/); 10 pt — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/help/artwork-and-design/line-art-and-text-recommendations-for-spot-uv-and-foil), though another 4over4 FAQ says 8 pt, with detail below about 6 pt breaking up — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/business-cards/how-small-can-foil-details-be-on-a-business-card) [snippet].
- Boxcar sets type loosely, keeps gaps to at least half the stock thickness, and flags as common mistakes tight tracking, 100% solid foil over 6 sq in, heavy coverage plus fine detail in one colour (may need two passes), and custom foil colours (generally not offered) — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963).
- Stock: 1-ply under 30 pt needs review for double-sided foil; 2-ply over 30 pt generally works with minimized impression — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963). Smooth uncoated works, heavy texture gives patchy adhesion, and thin-stroked serifs such as Didot and Bodoni break down at small sizes — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/).
- Foil is its own colour, printed on top and obscuring what is beneath. MOO says to give it a different shape from the artwork below to avoid a messy halo — [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines).
- Die cost: $30 to 60 for a simple logo of about 6 x 1.2 in, $100 to 200+ for intricate full coverage; dies stored up to 2 months — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/).

**Cold foil**
- UV adhesive is printed where foil is wanted, foil is pressed on and cured, typically inline, and no die is needed. It adheres most commonly to coated paper, and deep cold-foil colours can absorb UV and cause curing problems — search synthesis of [John S. Byrne](https://johnsbyrne.com/blog/helpful-facts-hot-foil-stamping-cold-foil-printing/), [INX](https://www.inxinternational.com/blog/shelf-appeal/mastering-art-foil-printing-complete-guide-hot-and-cold-techniques), [Duplo](https://www.duplousa.com/2025/09/hot-foil-vs-cold-foil-which-is-right-for-your-business/) [snippet].
- Sources conflict on detail, with each process's vendors claiming it holds finer lines — [Resource Label](https://www.resourcelabel.com/blog/2022/05/21/cold-foil-vs-hot-foil-for-labels/), [OnlineLabels](https://www.onlinelabels.com/articles/hot-foil-vs-cold-foil-are-you-using-the-right-method) [snippet].

**Blind deboss and emboss**
- Minimum line 0.6 mm (1.7 pt), depth 0.2 to 0.8 mm, stock at or above 250 gsm; thin stock shows through to the back and highly reflective stocks lose the shadow — [XC Paperbox](https://xcpaperbox-com-2ws.pages.dev/finishes/debossing/). Another guide says at least 300 gsm — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/emboss-vs-deboss/) [snippet].
- Die metals: magnesium is single-level, about 10,000 impressions, and one misfeed can ruin it. Copper is single-level, about 100,000 impressions. Brass is required for multi-level, sculpted and foil-emboss dies and takes a few weeks. Example cost: $50 small magnesium, $300+ brass — [Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/). Debossing die cost $150 to 400 — [XC Paperbox](https://xcpaperbox-com-2ws.pages.dev/finishes/debossing/).
- Very deep dies need bevelled edges of about 30 to 60 degrees to avoid cutting through. Raster art gives jagged die edges. Heat can scorch dark stocks. One single-level die cannot serve both large areas and fine detail. Embossing is the last finishing step, and foil heat can melt a laminate under a combination die — [Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/).
- Zeven's depth figure is internally inconsistent ("15 to 25 microns, averaging about 1/64 inch"; 1/64 in is about 400 microns). Do not use it — [Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/).
- Cotton is said to give crisper impressions than coated paper — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/emboss-vs-deboss/) [snippet].

**Thermography**
- Sheet prints normally, resin powder dusts onto wet ink, excess is vacuumed, heat melts it into a raised glossy layer. Body type from about 8 pt, rules at 0.5 pt or heavier. Hairlines, fine small serifs, tight reversed type, gradients and photos do not raise well. Laser printer fusers can soften the resin — [4over4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-is-thermography-printing-and-how-raised-ink-works).
- Fine powder suits small type and coarse powder suits solids, so the two conflict on one form — [Fine Print School](https://www.fineprintschool.com/thermography), [Briar Press](https://briarpress.org/18801) [snippet].
- It leaves a flat back and is the low-prestige imitation of engraving, which holds finer detail at the highest cost — [4over4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-is-thermography-printing-and-how-raised-ink-works).

**Spot UV**
- Minimum 10 pt type and 1 pt lines — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/help/artwork-and-design/line-art-and-text-recommendations-for-spot-uv-and-foil); 14 pt+ type, on coated stock of 350 gsm or more over matte laminate — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=1404) [snippet].
- Registration about ±0.5 mm — [Tradeprint](https://replatform.tradeprint.co.uk/business-card-printing/spot-uv-business-cards) [snippet].
- Forum practitioners: avoid positive lines under 1 mm (0.25 mm achievable) and reversed lines under 2 mm (1 mm achievable), allow 0.5 to 2 mm misregistration, keep at least 5 mm from trim. These are anecdotal — [PrintPlanet](https://printplanet.com/threads/the-limits-of-spot-uv.284838/).

**Soft-touch lamination**
- A soft-touch surface BOPP film applied by heat and pressure. Slightly more prone to fingerprints and scuffing than gloss or matte, with slightly less protection — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/soft-touch-lamination/).
- Burnishing flattens the texture into glossy patches at corners and edges, is physical and irreversible, and wiping enlarges it. Fingerprints leave dark or shiny patches. Large dark solids make all of this more conspicuous. Laminate can lift at its edge — [MyBookQuality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/materials/anti-scuff-soft-touch-finishes.html).
- Foil adhesion on soft-touch can be less consistent because the texture reduces contact area, and spot UV failures show at zone edges — [MyBookQuality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/materials/anti-scuff-soft-touch-finishes.html). Vendors counter that it "works beautifully" with foil and spot UV, without technical detail — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/soft-touch-lamination/).

**Edge painting**
- 32 pt is the practical minimum; 14 pt barely shows an edge, and 38 to 48 pt gives a wider band. Two thin coats, tight clamped stack. Failures: pooled paint gluing cards, seepage onto faces, chipping on coated stock, oil paint warping cards. Uncoated grips paint but can let colour soak into fibres — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/guide/how-to-make-painted-edge-business-cards).
- Keep a 1/8 in foil-free margin on all sides when edge painting or foil edging is planned — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963).
- Minimum 250 cards per design because a stacked batch is painted at once — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/help/what-is-the-minimum-quantity-for-edge-painted-business-cards.md) [snippet].
- Hand application means slight variation along the edge — search synthesis [Catdi](https://www.catdi.com/printing/business-cards/painted-edge-business-cards) [snippet].

**Edge gilding**
- Cards are stacked and clamped square so the edges become one flat surface; typically 32 pt or heavier so foil has enough surface — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/faq/what-is-edge-gilding-on-business-cards.md) [snippet].
- After Hours sands edges, applies foil with a heated roller by hand, duplexes up to 1000 gsm, and warns that rushing a stage makes foil flake — [After Hours](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/blog/post/how-to-foil-edge-gild-business-cards) [snippet]. 4over4 says it will not chip in normal use when bonded properly — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/general/will-the-gold-or-silver-edge-chip-over-time) [snippet].

**Die cutting**
- Minimum corner radius on a die cut 3 mm, acute angles 5 degrees — [Printing.com](https://www.printing.com/uk/file-supply/5355) [snippet]. Steel rule is 2 pt (0.71 mm) or 3 pt (1.07 mm) thick — search synthesis [Phillips Graphic Finishing](https://phillipsgraphicfinishing.squarespace.com/services/die-making) [snippet].
- Die placement drift about 1/16 in for the best shops — search synthesis [Qin Printing](https://qinprinting.com/die-cut) [snippet].

**Laser cutting and engraving on paper**
- Fast passes vaporize paper into smoke that carries heat away; air assist and exhaust reduce burning — [Trotec](https://www.troteclaser.com/en-gb/learn-support/helpcenter/how-to-laser-process-paper) [snippet].
- Features under 1 mm are fragile — [Ponoko](https://help.ponoko.com/en/articles/2688741-how-do-i-design-for-highest-quality) [snippet]. Bristol board cuts with a singed yellow-brown edge — [Cut Laser Cut](https://cutlasercut.com/materials/white-card/) [snippet].
- A hobbyist rule of thumb is 1.5 mm minimum cut feature with 2.5 mm between cuts; this is not a published spec — [Glowforge community](https://community.glowforge.com/t/wedding-invites/24?page=2) [snippet].

**Relative cost signal**
- US list prices at one vendor: standard cards $17.57, raised foil from $86.78, raised spot UV from $108.75, letterpress from $251.36 (15 business days, 32 or 48 pt) — [4over4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-is-thermography-printing-and-how-raised-ink-works).

### Inferences
- For the "no gloss" brief: thermography and spot UV are out because both are shiny by nature. Metallic foil and gilding are reflective, so whether a small metallic detail counts as gloss is a call for the owner. Matte pigment foil, blind deboss and painted edges fit cleanly.
- Soft-touch on a full navy flood is a poor pairing: dark solids show burnishing and fingerprints the most, and business cards get handled and carried. An uncoated cotton stock avoids the laminate entirely.
- A coral painted edge on a navy or white card is the only finish here that delivers the coral with exact ink-style colour on thick stock, with no reflective shine. It needs 32 pt or more and a 3 mm clear margin on the faces.
- What reads as cheap (combining sources above): thin stock with foil, thermography imitating engraving, halo from foil registered tightly to print, burnished laminate corners, and paint seeping onto faces.
- Safe design floors for this card: 1 pt lines and 8 pt type for foil, 1.7 pt (0.6 mm) for deboss, 0.5 pt lines and 8 pt type for letterpress.
- Finishing order, combining the sources: print, then laminate if any, then foil, then emboss or deboss last, then trim and edge work. Only "embossing last" is directly sourced.

### Gaps
- No Indian rupee rates for plates, dies, foil or edge work were found. Itemized quotes are needed. Dollar figures are US or China vendor numbers.
- Mama's Sauce, Foil Stampers and Kurz technical pages did not surface usable specs. Typoretum's foil page was a 404.
- No published minimum for material width between a die cut-out and the card edge, and no print-shop tolerance spec for laser engraving on paper.
- Soft-touch film thickness and any colour shift from lamination on navy were not found.
- No controlled test of edge-paint durability or gilding failure rates; all are vendor statements.

## 2. Minimum positive and reversed type and hairlines by process; reversed white on navy flood

### Takeaway
Offset holds 0.25 pt positive strokes, digital about 0.5 pt, and letterpress 0.25 to 0.35 pt. Reversed work should have at least 0.5 pt thinnest strokes on any process, and letterpress reversed type should be 12 pt or larger. On a navy flood, use bold sans at about 8 to 10 pt or more and no hairlines. Size and weight limits are tighter if the navy is built from several screened CMYK plates.

### Cited Findings
- Offset positive type: thinnest stroke 0.25 pt. Digital positive: about 0.5 pt or thicker unless noted. Reversed: 0.5 pt minimum regardless of process. 6 pt is typically legible on a standard business card — [Jukebox](https://support.jukeboxprint.com/en/articles/4214205-what-is-the-smallest-font-size-i-should-use).
- Another Jukebox article is cited as giving 0.3 pt for reversed strokes, so the printer's own numbers differ — [Jukebox](https://support.jukeboxprint.com/en/articles/3190067-what-is-the-smallest-font-size-i-should-use) [snippet].
- MOO's minimums for its finish cards are 10 pt text and 1 pt lines — [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines).
- Letterpress reversed: 12 pt or larger, varying by typeface, with a small stroke to offset ink gain. If a same-colour solid surrounds reversed text it may need a separate run — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/).
- Boxcar adds at least 0.75 pt extra stroke around reversed designs, on top of the minimum line or dot thickness, and also checks the dark gaps between white elements against plate minimums — [Boxcar reverse type](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=12684).
- Reversed type under 12 pt can clog on letterpress — [Boxcar search result](https://www.boxcarpress.com/faqs/category/designing-for-letterpress) [snippet].
- Foil reversed: 10 pt minimum, and reverses with fine detail need a separate die and run — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963).
- General reversed guidance ranges from 10 pt sans up to 14 pt for serifs, 10 to 12 pt for relatively bold sans — [Paper Mill Store](https://blog.thepapermillstore.com/design-techniques-reversed-type), [Greener Printer](https://greenerprinterhelp.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/63000281711-what-is-reversed-type-) [snippet; page fetch failed, per-source attribution unverified].
- Rich dark backgrounds fill knockouts: even slight misalignment gives poor edge definition on small type and thin lines. A build with a 100% black component creates a spread trap (Lane example 50/40/30/100) — [Lane Press](https://lanepress.com/?p=1504) [snippet].

### Inferences
- On press, spread narrows reversed strokes and misregistration colours their edges. A spot navy has one plate and no halftone edge, so it holds reversed type far better than a process build. See section 4.
- Practical targets for a navy flood: offset with spot navy, bold or medium sans at 7 to 8 pt or more with 0.5 pt+ strokes; offset or digital with CMYK navy, 8 to 10 pt, medium weight. These are my judgment from the ranges above, so test on a proof. Regular or light weights of a typeface at 6 pt reversed are unsafe on any process.
- Hindi and Punjabi scripts (Devanagari, Gurmukhi) carry thin strokes and modifiers, so if used, they should be sized up. No source covered this; it is general reasoning.

### Gaps
- No source separated reversed limits for coated versus uncoated stock numerically, and no digital-only reversed type size was found.
- No Indian printer publishes these minimums in the sources reached.

## 3. Sizes, bleed, safe margin, cut tolerance, and risky borders

### Takeaway
Use 89 x 51 mm trim, 3 mm bleed (95 x 57 mm file), and a 4 to 5 mm safe margin. Expect up to 1 mm cut variance, and avoid thin borders near the trim, since they make any shift visible.

### Cited Findings
- Vistaprint India's standard trimmed card is 89 x 51 mm (8.9 x 5.1 cm) — [Vistaprint India](https://www.vistaprint.in/visiting-cards/rounded-corner).
- 3 mm bleed gives a 95 x 57 mm file, 3 mm safe margin gives an 83 x 45 mm safe area; another Indian printer advises text 4 mm inside trim — search synthesis [SA Creative](https://sacreativestore.lovable.app/blog/visiting-card-sizes-dimensions), [Printster](https://printster.in/print/blog/how-to-print-visiting-cards-online) [snippet].
- Cut deviation up to 1 mm; 2 mm bleed and 4 mm safety margin by default — [Onlineprinters](https://onlineprinters.ie/c/faq/cutting-tolerances).
- MOO's bleed-size file is 88 x 59 mm — [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines).
- Keep foil 3 mm from trim, or give it 3 mm bleed if it runs off — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/).
- Spot UV: at least 5 mm from trim, per one forum practitioner — [PrintPlanet](https://printplanet.com/threads/the-limits-of-spot-uv.284838/).
- One printer places its cutting margins between 1/8 and 1/16 in inside the full-bleed line, and attributes misaligned white borders to cutting tolerance — [Overnight Prints](https://overnightprints.com/file-specs/guidelines) [snippet].
- Both faces need their own bleed and safe zones — [CheapFastPrinting](https://www.cheapfastprinting.com/blog/?p=39646) [snippet].

### Inferences
- A border 3 mm from trim with a 1 mm cut shift reads as 2 mm on one side and 4 mm on the other, a 2:1 visible error. The same shift on a 5 mm margin reads as 4 and 6 mm. Avoid thin keylines that echo the card edge, or set them 6 mm or more in. Full-bleed colour hides drift, which suits a navy flood.
- File sizes for the alternates by arithmetic: 90 x 54 becomes 96 x 60 mm; 85 x 55 becomes 91 x 61 mm.
- 89 x 51 is the safest specification for an Indian order because it is the one size an Indian printer's page confirmed.
- Live area at 4 mm: 81 x 43 mm. At 5 mm: 79 x 41 mm.

### Gaps
- No Indian source confirmed 90 x 54 or 85 x 55 as local standards. Printstop's page returned no body text and Jukebox's India size pages returned 403. Printo specs were not found.
- A named Indian printer's cut tolerance was not found; the 1 mm figure is a European online printer's.

## 4. Colour: Pantone vs CMYK, matching navy and coral, ink limits, foil

### Takeaway
Specify both colours as spots. Screen-computed nearest matches: coral **7416 C** (ΔE00 3.99) and **7579 U** (2.86); navy **533 C or 534 C** (about 4.5), with **282 U or 289 U** the closest uncoated (about 9.8). That means uncoated Pantones cannot reach #20334B's darkness on screen values, so navy on uncoated cotton needs a custom ink mix or a dyed stock. A coral foil is achievable only as a pigment foil, by stock find or custom order, and will not match the ink exactly.

Targets: #20334B is Lab 20.8 / 0.4 / -17.2; #EE7555 is Lab 63.0 / 44.2 / 39.2.

**CMYK**
- Naive formula conversion of #EE7555 is C0 M51 Y64 K7, reproduced by hand from RGB 238/117/85 and shown with no print profile — [Brandkit](https://brandkit-4m0.pages.dev/color/EE7555). The same formula gives #20334B as C57 M32 Y0 K71 (my calculation). Neither is a press value.

**Ink limits and dot gain**
- PSO Coated v3 (Fogra51): maximum TAC 330% — [Magno Gloss datasheet](https://igepa.be/media/24/30/81/1711070171/01-0000-4086_Magno-Gloss-Prepress-Datasheet.pdf) [snippet].
- PSOuncoated v3 (Fogra52): TAC 300%, max black 96%, ISO 12647-2:2013 PS 5 wood-free uncoated — [ICC registry](https://registry.color.org/profile-registry/PSOuncoated_v3_FOGRA52) [snippet]; substrate 120 g/m² — [color.org](https://color.org/chardata/FOGRA52.xalter) [snippet].
- Uncoated stock needs more dot gain compensation than coated, per an anecdotal forum post — [PrintPlanet](https://printplanet.com/threads/gracol-and-uncoated.4791/) [snippet].
- Rich black reference: 60/40/40/100, total 240% — [Pure Buttons](https://purebuttons.com/faq/achieve-true-rich-black-print) [snippet]. Rules of thumb for safe coverage range from 240% to 300% — search synthesis [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/cmyk-values-for-print-explained.md), [Printpapa](https://support.printpapa.com/kb/a410/plain-black-vs-rich-black.aspx) [snippet].
- Small text should use a single ink, as misregistration blurs multi-ink edges — [MyBookQuality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/printing/color-to-color-misregister.html) [snippet].

**Foil colour**
- Pigment foil lays down dense, opaque colour with sharp edges in one pass, in matt or gloss, and Foilco offers more than 40 pigment foils — [People of Print on Foilco](https://peopleofprint.com/product/you-dont-have-to-shine-to-stand-out-foilco-on-the-art-of-pigment-foil/).
- API's PG series is matt pigment hot stamping foil for both fine detail and broad coverage — [API via Packaging & Labelling](https://www.packaging-labelling.com/products/api-foilmakers-limited/pigment-hot-stamping-foils) [snippet].
- Crown Roll Leaf's lab formulates custom colours from Pantone shades or physical samples — [Crown Roll Leaf](https://crownrollleaf.com/color-matching/). No MOQ or lead time is published. Boxcar generally does not offer custom foil colours — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963).
- Even a foil maker's own Pantone-referenced foils carry the caveat that results vary when applied to card — [Entrust](https://www.entrust.com/sites/default/files/documentation/datasheets/graphics-ribbons-topping-foils-ds.pdf) [snippet].
- Foil is far more opaque than letterpress ink, which suits light colour on dark stock — [Made Good Designs](https://madegooddesigns.com/foil-stamping/) [snippet].

### Inferences
- Navy on uncoated is the main colour risk. Uncoated simulations sit near L30 to L35 against the target's L21, so a standard U ink will read as a faded slate-indigo. Ways out: custom-mixed darker spot ink, double hit, navy-dyed or duplexed stock, or accept a lighter navy. Note that U hex values are simulations of ink on paper, so a physical drawdown is the real test.
- Hue check on coated: 533 C sits closest in lightness, 534 C is a little bluer, 2767 C and 289 C are darker and bluer, 7546 C greyer, 7463 C leans teal. For "steel navy" my starting shortlist is 533 C and 534 C, with 7546 C as the greyer option, and choosing needs a swatch book.
- 7416 C and U can differ; 7416 U is reported at #EA746A [snippet in search results], pinker than the brand coral, while 7579 U is closer in the dataset. Use the number that fits the stock actually chosen.
- A CMYK navy is dark in K and needs screened C and M. On uncoated stock a halftone flood looks mottled and loses edge definition in knockouts. Unless budget forces CMYK, run navy as a solid spot. A coral plate is a second spot. This also suits letterpress-style thinking of 1 to 2 solid inks with no tints, and fits the no-gradient brief.
- If CMYK is forced, have the printer convert with their own profile and keep TAC at or under 300% on uncoated. A 100K build would look black, so avoid borrowing rich-black recipes for navy.
- Foil cannot match coral exactly because it is a transferred pigment or metal film sold in fixed shades, with its own surface sheen, rather than a mixed ink absorbed by the paper. Pantone 7416 printed beside coral foil on the same card will show the difference, so use only one coral method per face.

### Gaps
- No Kurz page or stock list of an orange-coral matte pigment foil was found. Whether Indian foil suppliers stock or can order a coral pigment foil is unknown.
- No profile-based CMYK conversion could be done because no ICC profile tools were available here. No Pantone Bridge CMYK values were obtained. Pantone's finder page did not expose values to scripts, and Pantone Connect was not accessed.
- Numeric uncoated TVI at 40 or 50% tints were not verified. The ICC "TVI: CMYK 22%" entry is ambiguous.
- Whether Indian offset printers calibrate to Fogra 51/52 or similar is unknown.
- Pantone Coated and Uncoated hexes in the dataset are undated, so recent library revisions may differ.

## 5. Prepress file requirements

### Takeaway
Supply a vector PDF/X-4 with outlined fonts, 300 dpi images, 3 mm bleed, and each finish as a separate named 100% spot colour set to overprint, unless the printer's template says otherwise. Keep at least 0.5 to 1 mm clearance between foil and print rather than aligning them.

### Cited Findings
- PDF/X-4:2010 is recommended when a file needs spot colours because it "retains spot colours" — [Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/what-is-a-pdf-x1a-2001). Tradeprint treats X-1a as CMYK-focused, but other sources say X-1a keeps spots while flattening transparency and layers — [CCS Imaging Center](https://campus.collegeforcreativestudies.edu/imaging-center/2021/10/26/quick-tip-3/) [snippet].
- PDF/X-4 retains live transparency, layers and spot colours — [CCS Imaging Center](https://campus.collegeforcreativestudies.edu/imaging-center/2021/10/26/quick-tip-3/) [snippet].
- A foil spot should stay 100% or 0% with no grey shades, spot names must be exact, and renaming or transparency can cause problems — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-671/help-preparing-document-with-spot-colors-for-printing-on-heidelberg-versafire-873590) [snippet].
- One user found X-3 turned spots to overprint while X-4 did not, so check overprint in preflight — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/indesign-discussions/help-preparing-document-with-spot-colors-for-printing-on-heidelberg-versafire/m-p/12748419/highlight/true) [snippet].
- Conventions differ by printer. Tradeprint wants foil as 100% black on a separate page of a 4-page PDF, vector only — [Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork). MOO wants a black-and-white PDF finish file at bleed size, no crop marks, 300 dpi artwork — [MOO](https://www.moo.com/uk/design-templates/tailored-business-cards/guidelines).
- Boxcar: do not alter artwork to simulate overprinting; prepress applies trapping — [Boxcar](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=5963). Send right-reading artwork, outlined fonts, and do not pre-reverse — [Boxcar design tips](https://www.boxcarpress.com/letterpress-design-tips/).
- Indian printer guidance: print-ready PDF, 300 DPI, CMYK, 3 mm bleed, fonts converted to outlines — [Printster](https://printster.in/print/blog/how-to-print-visiting-cards-online) [snippet].
- Emboss dies need vector art because raster gives jagged die edges that can cut paper — [Zeven Design](https://zevendesign.com/graphic-designers-guide-embossing/).

### Inferences
- Name layers plainly, such as `FOIL_CORAL` and `DEBOSS`, and ask the Indian printer whether they want spot channels or separate files. Printers differ (see Tradeprint and MOO above), so the safe pack is both: a composite PDF/X-4 and one black-vector PDF per finish.
- Foil spot objects should overprint so nothing knocks out of the navy, or foil misregistration exposes white paper. Under opaque foil, the print below stays hidden if foil sits inside a solid.
- Include a mockup PDF showing the placement of each finish, so the finisher has a visual reference for position.

### Gaps
- ISO 15930 text was not consulted directly; PDF/X facts are from vendor and university pages.
- No source states a universally accepted foil spot name.
- No Indian printer's prepress spec for foil channels was found.

## 6. Registration tolerance between processes and front to back

### Takeaway
Plan for ±0.5 mm at best and 1.5 to 3 mm at worst between foil, deboss, spot UV or die and the printed image, and treat each process as independent. Do not rely on a front-to-back alignment or a precise relationship to the trim.

### Cited Findings
- Foil to print: ±0.5 mm — [Qin Printing](https://www.qinprinting.com/foil-stamping/); up to 1/16 in (1.59 mm), because foil runs on a separate machine; avoid tight registration and give foil breathing room — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/faq/what-should-i-know-about-foil-stamping-and-registration-accuracy.md); about 3 mm permissible — [Tradeprint](https://help.tradeprint.co.uk/how-to-set-up-a-metallic-foil-artwork); 1 to 3 mm — [Printpapa](https://support.printpapa.com/kb/a484/designers-guide-to-foil-based-artwork_.aspx) [snippet].
- Spot UV: about ±0.5 mm — [Tradeprint](https://replatform.tradeprint.co.uk/business-card-printing/spot-uv-business-cards) [snippet]; design for 1 mm, expect 0.5 to 2 mm — [PrintPlanet](https://printplanet.com/threads/the-limits-of-spot-uv.284838/) (forum).
- Cutting: up to 1 mm — [Onlineprinters](https://onlineprinters.ie/c/faq/cutting-tolerances).
- Front-to-back: tolerance can shift a design between sides when printing double-sided or cutting down, with no number given — [SAIC Service Bureau](https://sites.saic.edu/servicebureau/?p=885) [snippet].

### Inferences
- A 0.25 pt line (0.09 mm) or even a 1 pt line (0.35 mm) is thinner than the ±0.5 mm tolerance, so outlining a printed shape with foil or deboss will always show the error. Use foil shapes that stand alone or sit well inside a larger solid. Avoid foil keylines that trace a printed form, and avoid an inset deboss that frames print.
- Deboss aligned with a printed element (a "registered deboss") carries the same risk. A blind deboss of a logo on its own is safe.
- Deep deboss shows on the reverse. On 1-ply stock, keep the back clear behind it or use a duplexed sheet, per the show-through risk in section 1. Back-side content should not depend on the front's position.

### Gaps
- No source gave a numeric front-to-back register for offset, digital or letterpress on business cards.
- No numeric tolerance was found for deboss to print, and no Indian printer's figures were found.

## 7. QR codes and NFC as premium additions

### Takeaway
Print the QR flat in solid navy on a white or light panel, at about 20 to 25 mm, with a 4-module quiet zone and a short URL. Avoid foil, emboss and deboss for the code itself. NFC cards are PVC or metal products at CR80 size and not paper-card add-ons, so treat NFC as a separate object.

### Cited Findings
- Quiet zone: 4 modules on all sides, per ISO/IEC 18004 — [QRStuff](https://www.qrstuff.com/feeds/blog/gs1-qr-code-quiet-zone-4-modules) [snippet], [Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/).
- Module size: Scanova cites ISO 18004 as at least 0.25 mm, while another source says the standard sets no physical minimum and only module relationships — [Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/); [Linkbreakers](https://linkbreakers.com/help/article/qr-code-size-and-print-dimension-benchmarks) [snippet].
- Size: technical minimum about 1 x 1 cm for Version 1; under 2 x 2 cm carries high scan-failure risk; business cards 2.5 cm in its table and 0.8 in as a close-up minimum. A vCard code of about 57 rows came to 3.5 cm in its worked example — [Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/). A size of 2 cm is called the practical floor elsewhere — [WaveCnct](https://wavecnct.com/blogs/how-small-can-a-qr-code-be) [snippet]. One guide allows 12 x 12 mm — search synthesis [snippet, source not attributable].
- Scan distance ratio close to 10:1; avoid bumpy or reflective surfaces; matte and smooth work best — [Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/).
- Contrast thresholds conflict: 4:1, 4.5:1, and a "40% luminance" figure; an ISO/IEC 15415 grade A is said to need 70% or higher contrast. None was verified against the standard — search synthesis [Pageloot](https://pageloot.com/blog/best-practices-for-qr-code-readability/), [QR Codes Unlimited](https://qrcodesunlimited.com/academy/minimum-qr-code-size) [snippet].
- Inverted light-on-dark codes are permitted by the standard since its 2006 edition, but some scanner apps do not read them — search synthesis [Scanova](https://scanova.io/blog/minimum-qr-code-size/) [snippet].
- Metallic or raised foil QR codes will not scan reliably because the coating reflects light and disrupts contrast — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/finish/can-i-print-a-qr-code-with-metallic-or-raised-foil-stamping) [snippet].
- Embossing can distort edges enough to make a code unreliable, so keep the code flat and emboss the logo or border instead — [4over4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/business-cards/can-i-add-a-qr-code-to-an-embossed-gloss-business-card) [snippet].
- Gloss is riskier than matte for QR codes — [MOO support](https://support.moo.com/hc/en-gb/articles/7990289486996-Using-QR-codes-with-MOO) [snippet; page fetch returned 403].
- If the code must sit on dark stock, use a light silk screen or matte foil — [Jukebox help](https://intercom.help/jukeboxprint/en/articles/3189598-what-paper-stocks-can-i-print-a-qr-code-on) [snippet].

**NFC**
- NTAG213, 215 and 216 are NXP mass-market NFC Forum Type 2 ICs — [NXP datasheet](https://nxp.com/docs/en/data-sheet/NTAG213_215_216.pdf) [snippet]. Usable memory about 144, 504 and 888 bytes; NTAG213 suffices for a URL — search synthesis, [Mindware Indian RFID](https://www.indianrfid.com/nfc-213-card) [snippet].
- PVC card thickness about 0.76 to 0.88 mm, at 86 x 54 mm — [RFIDSolution datasheet](https://rfidsolution.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NFC-Business-Card-NXP-NTAG-213-86-54-0.88-mm-datasheet-RS-N213-CP-8654.pdf) [snippet].
- Metal NFC cards are CR80 and thicker because of the chip and antenna, with ferrite or anti-metal layers — [GoToTags](https://store.gototags.com/matte-black-metal-nfc-card-ntag213/) [snippet]. Read range is about 2 to 10 cm, and metal generally reads less well — search synthesis, vendor listings [snippet].
- Paper and PET coverings exist for 13.56 MHz print cards — [Asia RFID](https://www.asiarfid.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/NFC-Print-Card-with-Ntag213-Chip.pdf) [snippet].
- Indian supply: Mindware in New Delhi sells NFC 213 cards, with data retention up to 10 years — [Mindware](https://www.indianrfid.com/nfc-213-card) [snippet]. GMP N-TAG listings range ₹160 to ₹5,500 by chip and pack — [DDSKart](https://ddskart.com/product/gmp-13-56-mhz-rfid-proximity-cards-1k-cards/) [snippet].

### Inferences
- WCAG-style contrast by my calculation: navy on white 12.83:1, coral on navy 4.47:1, coral on white 2.87:1. Navy on white is the only strong pair for a QR. Coral on white fails even the loosest 4:1 vendor figure. Coral on navy would be inverted and marginal, so avoid it.
- A short URL fits about Version 3 (29 x 29 modules). At a 20 mm code this gives 0.69 mm modules plus 2.8 mm quiet zone per side, about 25.5 mm total footprint, which fits the 41 to 43 mm live height. A vCard at about 57 rows would drop to 0.35 mm modules at 20 mm, which is close to the printing limits on uncoated stock. Link to a short URL and not a vCard. Use a stable redirect, since cards outlive URLs.
- A blind deboss QR has no colour contrast, so it should be treated as non-scannable. Matte pigment foil in navy on white could work in theory because it is not reflective, but no source tested it. Plain ink is the safe option.
- On a navy flood back, a knocked-out white panel works, with panel margin acting as the quiet zone. Alternatively, keep the QR on a white face of the card.
- Embedding NFC in 600 gsm paper needs an inlay between plies, which suggests a custom duplex job; no source confirmed this for Indian paper-card printers. A deliberate separate NFC card for key staff is simpler.

### Gaps
- ISO/IEC 18004 is paywalled and was not read. All ISO claims are second-hand, and the module-minimum conflict is unresolved.
- No test data on QR reliability on uncoated cotton, letterpress ink or matte pigment foil.
- No read-range data for paper-embedded NFC, and no Indian supplier of NFC inlays inside premium paper cards was found.
- Current Indian NFC per-card prices for branded cards were not established.
