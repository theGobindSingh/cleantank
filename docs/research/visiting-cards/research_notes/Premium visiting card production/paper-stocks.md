# Premium visiting card substrates and physical construction

Researched 2026-11-01. Evidence quality: mill data sheets were hard to reach (Jukebox returned 403, Gmund's product URLs now redirect to its home page), so most numbers come from merchants and printers. Items marked "search digest" were seen only in a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened. Unit conversions are mine: 1 pt = 25.4 µm; 1 lb cover = 2.70 gsm.

## Which stocks do premium cards use, how do they feel and how do they take ink?

### Takeaway
Premium cards fall into three material families: true cotton (Crane Lettra, Conqueror Connoisseur, Gmund Cotton), dyed-through virgin wood-pulp uncoated (Colorplan, Keaykolour) and coated board with soft-touch film. Lettra suits deep letterpress and Colorplan is the colour reference; neither family is automatically better, because cotton buys softness and bulk while dyed pulp buys colour.

### Cited Findings
**Crane Lettra (cotton)**
- 100% cotton, described as strong and stable on press with a soft, luxurious feel; engineered for letterpress and also successfully offset-printed and engraved — [Legion Paper](https://www.legionpaper.com/cranes-lettra) (search digest)
- 220 lb double-thick cover is listed at 595 g/m², uncoated, long grain, caliper 40.3 — [The Paper Mill Store](https://www.thepapermillstore.com/crane-s-lettra-pearl-white-paper-8-1-2-x-11-in-220-lb-cover-dt-lettra-letterpress-100-cotton-50-per-package.html) (search digest); another seller rounds it to 600 gsm at the same 40.3 caliper — [Cardstock Warehouse](https://www.cardstock-warehouse.com/products/crane-lettra-pearl-white-sheets) (search digest)
- The sheet is uncalendared, leaving looser fibres and more texture — search digest, Lettra retailer listing above
- Its soft, pillowy surface "takes deep impression" and it is called the standard-bearer for letterpress, though this comes from third-party printer copy, not test data — [Boxcar Press](https://www.boxcarpress.com/?p=6832) (search digest, attribution uncertain)
- Lettra is sold through Mohawk's stock-check system — [Mohawk](https://www.mohawkconnects.com/stock-check/22419)

**Colorplan (dyed virgin pulp, not cotton)**
- 100% ECF virgin fibre, lignin-free, unbuffered; about 55 colours; listed for letterpress, offset, silkscreen and foil — [Legion Paper](https://legionpaper.com/colorplan)
- Germany's distributor lists 50 colours, 25 finishes and 8 grammages — [May+Spies](https://www.mayspies.com/en/Colorplan-paper/) (search digest)
- Fedrigoni is Colorplan's manufacturing partner — [PITA](https://www.pita.org.uk/what-we-do/news-services/1695-gf-smith-confirms-new-manufacturing-partner-for-colorplan) (search digest)

**Conqueror, Curious, Keaykolour, Rives (ex-Arjowiggins, now Antalis)**
- Antalis bought the Conqueror, Curious, Keaykolour, Pop'Set and Rives brands after Arjowiggins Scotland entered administration in September 2022; manufacture moved to a different European mill, with Antalis saying the papers were kept close to the originals — [PrintWeek](https://printweek.com/articles/antalis-buys-arjo-brands); [PrintWeek](https://printweek.com/articles/antalis-updates-on-arjo-range-revamp) (search digest)
- After relaunch, Conqueror keeps "the exact same recipe" in three finishes, six shades and optional watermark; Keaykolour continues in 45 colours — [PrintWeek](https://printweek.com/articles/revamped-conqueror-is-relaunched) (search digest). Older copy says 48 colours — [Pulp and Paper Technology](https://www.pulpandpaper-technology.com/products/arjo-wiggin-fine-papers/creative-papers) (search digest)
- Conqueror Connoisseur is a 100% cotton grade, alongside Wove, Laid and CX22 — [PrintWeek India](https://printweek.in/features/sona-papers-seven-wonders-noel-dcunha-sunday-column-28718?amp=1)
- Keaykolour: natural, wove-textured, high-rigidity papers and boards, with 100% recycled options — [Pulp and Paper Technology](https://www.pulpandpaper-technology.com/products/arjo-wiggin-fine-papers/creative-papers) (search digest)
- Rives: highly textured, eight textures, ten colours, 80 to 350 gsm — search digest, same source
- Curious Matter: a surface "between silk and sand", made with potato starch — [PaperSpecs](https://paperspecs.com/?p=21152) (search digest)

**Gmund Cotton**
- Listed at 110, 300, 600 and 900 g/m² in 70 × 100 cm sheets, matt finish, with the 900 g/m² listing suggesting business cards — [Gmund](https://world-en.gmund.com/gmund-cotton-new-grey-900-g-m2-70-0-cm-x-100-0-cm/71-bw61-900-70-100) (search digest; URL now redirects)

**Mohawk Superfine**
- Described as ultra-smooth and refined; one printer recommends it when little impression is needed — [Briar Press](https://briarpress.org/13857) (search digest)
- Mohawk joined the Fedrigoni group in 2024 — [PrintWeek MENA, Dec 2024](https://printweekmena.com/newsimage/monthly_flipbooks/Dec_2024/files/basic-html/page23.html)

**Soft-touch laminated board**
- A thin matte polypropylene film heat-bonded over the printed sheet; velvety, slight rubbery grip, no glare; best on 16 pt or thicker — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md)
- Pen ink slides off the film, so laminate one side if the back must be writable — same source

### Inferences
- Lettra is much bulkier per gram than Colorplan. Lettra 595 gsm at 1,024 µm gives 1.72 cm³/g; Colorplan 702 gsm at 965 µm gives 1.37 cm³/g. A cotton card therefore feels thicker and softer at a lower gsm, and gsm comparisons across families mislead.
- Soft-touch reads expensive at first touch, but the hand is touching plastic film. The paper's own texture is hidden, so stock choice stops mattering once film goes on.
- Keaykolour's rigidity and Conqueror Connoisseur's cotton are the most relevant Antalis grades, given Sona's Indian distribution (see the India section).

### Gaps
- No mill data sheet was opened for Lettra, Gmund Cotton, Mohawk Superfine, Curious Matter, Conqueror or Keaykolour.
- Gmund Cotton's exact cotton percentage, and Mohawk Superfine's fibre content, were not confirmed. Superfine's 160 lb DTC caliper was not found.
- No measured impression-depth comparison between Lettra and Superfine exists in the sources found.
- Ink-holdout data (dot gain, drying) by stock was not found.

## What GSM ranges read as standard, premium and luxury, and when does extra thickness stop helping?

### Takeaway
350 gsm is the professional default, 400 gsm a small upgrade, about 540 to 700 gsm the true premium step, and 900 gsm and up starts to read as novelty. Caliper matters more than gsm: aim for about 700 to 1,000 µm for a founder card.

### Cited Findings
- Below about 300 gsm feels flimsy; 350 gsm is where most quality cards sit; 400 gsm feels more substantial and is "a small upgrade that gets noticed" — [Xcel UK](https://xceluk.com/business-card-paper-weights-finishes/) (search digest, attribution uncertain)
- Single-ply standard is 350 to 400 gsm; duplex 600 to 700 gsm "does not flex" and feels like a hotel key card; triplex 900 to 1,000 gsm has "tile-like rigidity" — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)
- Triplex is less common and its weight can read as "novelty" rather than "premium" — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)
- A 20-card holder takes roughly 10 to 12 duplex cards — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)
- Thick cards can feel out of place in casual settings, and standard cards suit volumes of 5,000 or more — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)
- Colorplan caliper by US basis weight: 100# cover 14.4 pt, 130# cover 18.5 pt, 200# cover 28 pt, 260# cover 38 pt — [Legion Paper](https://legionpaper.com/colorplan)
- Colorplan 270 gsm is listed at 360 µm — [Fred Aldous](https://www.fredaldous.co.uk/products/card-a4-colorplan-270gsm-8-pack)
- Colorplan is sold as 350, 540, 700, 810, 1,050 and 1,400 gsm business cards by one in-house duplexing studio — [After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/faqs/general/how-thick-is-colorplan-paper-for-business-cards)
- Coated stock conversions quoted: 300 gsm ≈ 14 pt, 350 gsm ≈ 16 pt, 400 gsm ≈ 18 pt — [Overnight Prints](https://blog.overnightprints.com/business-card-paper-thickness-guide/) (search digest, attribution uncertain). Sources note gsm measures weight, not thickness.
- One supplier goes up to 1,190 gsm — search digest, source not pinned down
- Indian mainstream online printers typically offer 300 to 400 gsm — [PrintStop](https://www.printstop.co.in/blog/best-visiting-card-printing-companies-india) (vendor blog, search digest)
- One Indian vendor positions 600 gsm cotton as its luxury tier, suited to letterpress, foil and edge painting — [SA Creative](https://sacreativestore.lovable.app/services/business-cards) (low-trust vendor page)

| Stock | gsm | Caliper | Source of caliper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Colorplan | 270 | 366 µm (14.4 pt) | Legion; Fred Aldous gives 360 µm |
| Colorplan | 351 | 470 µm (18.5 pt) | Legion, converted |

| Colorplan | 702 | 965 µm (38 pt) | Legion, converted |

### Inferences
- Practical tiers for India: 300 to 350 gsm is commodity, 400 gsm is ordinary premium, 540 to 700 gsm dyed or 600 gsm cotton is real premium, 800 to 1,000 gsm is statement only.
- Extra thickness stops helping at around 1 mm. By then the card no longer flexes at all, so more plies add wallet bulk and cost without changing the feel. The sources support the novelty risk and wallet effect but give no exact threshold.
- A card about 1 mm thick is roughly two to three times a normal card, which is why 700 gsm is a sensible upper limit for a card meant to be handed out daily.
- Staff cards handed out in volume are better at 350 to 540 gsm; thickness is worth paying for mainly on founder cards.

### Gaps
- No perception research or user testing on gsm tiers was found; all tier language is printer marketing.
- No Indian-market source defines tiers beyond vendor copy.

## Duplex, triplex and multiloft: what are they, how are they glued, and when is a coloured core worthwhile?

### Takeaway
Duplex and triplex bond two or three finished sheets, usually with roller-applied cold glue, then trim after curing. A coloured core is worth it when the edge is a deliberate brand moment and the cards will be seen edge-on; for Clean Tank that means a coral seam on the founder card at most.

### Cited Findings
- Sequence: each sheet prints flat, adhesive goes on one face, a mounting press joins the sheets and pushes out air, the bond cures, and only then is the card trimmed. Trimming last avoids a lip from misaligned halves — [4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them)
- Cold glue on a roller for most work; heat-activated film for laminated or coated stock that glue struggles to wet — same source
- Risks: a scored duplex card cracks along the glue line; adhesive can creep in a hot car or humid mailbox; uncoated fibres absorb humidity, which can lift a corner; laminated pairs resist this better — same source
- Two 16 pt sheets give about 32 pt; two 14 pt sheets about 28 pt; silk-laminated pairs of 16 pt about 37 pt; laminate film adds about 1.5 mil per face — same source
- Allow up to 7 days for adhesive to cure fully; machine limit SRA3 at one UK studio; quadplexing four 250 gsm sheets gives 1,000 gsm — [After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/us/faqs/production/thickest-business-card)
- A common triplex build is 250 gsm silk, 300 gsm coloured core and 250 gsm silk, totalling 800 gsm — [After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/portfolio/triple-thick-800gsm-gold-foil-business-card/) (search digest)
- Thick coloured cores go up to 500 gsm, for cards near 1,000 gsm — [After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/us/faqs/production/thickest-business-card)
- Duplex runs 28 to 42 pt; triplex 42 to 63 pt (three-ply starts at 42 pt for Colorplan, 63 pt for cotton); duplexing adds a day or two to production; each side can use a different colour or stock — [Dolce Press](https://dolcepress.com/finishing/duplexing)
- Triplex shows two bond lines at the edge rather than one; the core is visible mainly edge-on, so treat it as "a tactile surprise" rather than main branding — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)
- Some colour-core cards sandwich white sticker paper over Colorplan so the print carries no colour cast — [Aura Print](https://aura-print.com/ca/triplex-business-card-printing) (search digest)
- Duplex pricing example: from $2.27 per card against $0.08 for standard, inc. GST, currency not stated but probably AUD (Paperlust's own figures conflict slightly) — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=3316)

### Inferences
- India's heat and monsoon humidity make the glue-creep and corner-lift risks real for an uncoated duplex card. Ask the printer about adhesive and allow full cure before guillotining.
- Coral core between navy faces gives the strongest brand match available, because #EE7555 sits at the edge and never competes with the face. It needs a coral dyed sheet, and the two bond lines add production risk.
- Never score a duplex card. Any fold design should be dropped.
- If a coloured core is out of reach, a coral painted edge gives a similar effect on uncoated stock.

### Gaps
- "Multiloft" did not appear as a defined term in any source read. It appears to be a trade name for pasted multi-ply board, but this could not be confirmed.
- Whether Colorplan 540 and 700 gsm are single-ply or pasted at the mill was not established. Legion lists them as weights; After Hours says it duplexes in-house.
- No Indian source describes adhesive method or cure time. No delamination rate data exists.

## Dyed-through stock versus printed flood colour: which holds up better for dark navy, and what navy stocks exist?

### Takeaway
Dyed-through stock is the better choice for a navy card, because the colour runs through the sheet and no white core appears at the cut edge. Unlaminated flood print running to the trim is what cracks. Soft-touch film on flood navy is the durable second choice but is a plastic surface and resists pens.

### Cited Findings
- Edge cracking is linked to heavy, dark, full-bleed colours: extra ink saturates fibres and makes coated surfaces brittle where cut or folded — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/faqs/marketing-materials/why-does-my-business-card-crack-around-the-edges) (search digest)
- Cracking usually hits a small share of a run; lamination protects edges during cutting; on non-laminated cards avoid heavy ink at the edges — [Primoprint](https://support.primoprint.com/articles/1427030-cracking) (search digest)
- Soft-touch deepens navy and charcoal, resists fingerprints and scuffs, and blacks should be proofed because the film shifts dark colours — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md) (vendor copy)
- Contradicting that: matte film shows scuffing and burnishing more readily than gloss, with friction leaving irreversible shiny patches; anti-scuff soft-touch grades resist better; soft-touch is more prone to stress cracking at folds, shown as white lines on dark areas — [Book Quality](https://mybookquality-website.pages.dev/materials/laminates.html) (book-cover context, by analogy, search digest)
- Dark ink pigment can be scuffed by fingers or pens, especially on coated paper — [HP support](https://support.hp.com/hr-en/document/ish_7630461-2242793-16) (search digest, inkjet context)

**Navy stocks found**
- Colorplan Imperial Blue is a named colour; it appears in US swatches as "Imperial Blue" and in Legion's range as "Loch (Imperial)". Other dark blues listed: Royal Blue, Azure, Cobalt, Sapphire, New Blue, Tabriz Blue — [Cards & Pockets](https://cardsandpockets.com/Imperial-Blue/); [Legion Paper](https://legionpaper.com/colorplan). Note that some of those are not dark.
- Keaykolour Original Navy Blue at 250 gsm, one shade described as very dark navy; Royal Blue at 250 and 300 gsm, with one 450 gsm card — [Paperglitz](https://www.paperglitz.com.au/catalog/keaykolour-original-navy-blue-250gsm-card-a4-sheets.html); [The Envelope People](https://www.theenvelopepeople.co.uk/?p=171683) (search digest)
- Gmund Cotton Gentlemen Blue at 300 g/m² — [Gmund](https://world-en.gmund.com/gmund-cotton-gentlemen-blue-300-g-m2-70-0-cm-x-100-0-cm/71-bw62-300-70-100) (search digest; URL now redirects)
- Gmund Colors Matt is numbered and listed at 60 to 350 g/m² in the results seen, with no 700 g/m² sheet — [Gmund](https://world-en.gmund.com/gmund-colors-matt-49-350-g-m2-100-0-cm-x-70-0-cm/71-c49-350-100-70) (search digest)

### Inferences
- On a printed navy card, a white core is what shows when the edge chips or cracks. A dyed sheet has no such contrast, so wear reads as patina rather than damage.
- A dyed stock cannot take CMYK or black type legibly, so all type must be foil, white ink or blind. That is a large constraint on staff cards with variable data, and an argument for white stock with navy print for staff.
- Uncoated navy will show oily handling marks and will rub slowly over time, which no source quantified. Foil on dyed uncoated navy is the most robust option for type.
- A matte aqueous or soft-touch coat on the navy side is the fallback if flood printing is chosen. Brand rules forbid gloss, so ask for anti-scuff soft-touch and no spot UV.
- Flood printing gives exact #20334B control; a dyed stock is whatever the mill made. A swatch-book check against #20334B is required before committing.

### Gaps
- I found no source on fingerprint visibility on dyed uncoated dark stock, and no controlled scuff test comparing dyed stock with flood print. The conclusion above rests on the mechanism, not on test data.
- No published Lab or hex values for Imperial Blue, Keaykolour Navy Blue or Gentlemen Blue, so match to #20334B is unverified.
- Jukebox's Imperial Blue page (403) was not readable. A search digest said a UK stockist lists Imperial Blue in 700 gsm, but I could not identify the page, so availability at 540 and 700 gsm needs confirming.
- Gentlemen Blue at 600 g/m² not confirmed.

## Corners and edge finishing: square versus rounded, what radius, and which edge choices suit which stock?

### Takeaway
Square corners stay the safer premium default on thick uncoated stock, and 3 mm is the radius to use if rounding. Painted edges need uncoated stock. On dyed navy the bare edge is already finished, so paint only for a coral accent.

### Cited Findings
- 1/8 inch equals 3.175 mm; 1/8 and 3/8 inch are 4OVER4's standard options, with other radii custom — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/help/what-is-the-difference-between-3-8-1-4-and-1-8-rounded-corners.md) (search digest)
- Other printers default differently: 1/4 and 3/8 inch templates at Printing For Less — [Printing For Less](https://printingforless.com/template/rounded-corner-templates) (search digest)
- Trim tolerance is cited at 1/32 inch to about 1/16 inch, so keep content clear of curves — same source; [Formax Printing](https://www.formaxprinting.com/blog/printing-lingo-what-does-radius-mean-when-referring-to-rounded-corners) (search digest, attribution uncertain)
- Rounded corners use standard dies, unlike custom shapes — [Rise and Shine](https://rise-and-shine-2026.pages.dev/how-we-print/die-cutting) (search digest)
- Painted edges go on after trimming, need uncoated stock (paint won't grip laminated faces), and came in 16 colours at one vendor — [4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/what-are-duplex-business-cards-and-when-to-use-them); contradicted by the same vendor saying edge painting works with soft-touch — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/.well-known/okf/guide/what-are-soft-touch-laminated-business-cards.md)
- Rounded corners are offered with painted edges at some printers — [Gold Image Printing](https://goldimageprinting.com/Edge-Painted-Business-Cards.html) (search digest); one printer will not edge paint die-cut pieces — [Pearl Grant](https://pearlgrant.com/pages/designers-fine-press-edge-treatments) (search digest, attribution uncertain)
- On a 32 pt duplex, the painted band is twice as tall and reads as a deliberate feature — [BQP](https://bqp-storefront.pages.dev/blog/painted-edge-business-cards-when-32pt-is-worth-it) (search digest)
- Edge colouring pairs well with duplexed cards — [Dolce Press](https://dolcepress.com/finishing/duplexing)
- Edge gilding is a common reason to order 1,000 gsm cards — [After Hours Creative](https://www.afterhourscreativestudio.com/us/faqs/production/thickest-business-card)

### Inferences
- A 3 mm radius relates well to a thick card; 1/4 inch (6.35 mm) looks soft and consumer. Small radius also matches a precise, technical brand.
- Rounded corners on heavy duplex stress the glue line less than sharp corners, which may help with corner lift in humidity. That is reasoning only; no source tests it.
- Round before painting. Otherwise confirm the printer's order of operations.
- Edge options by stock: dyed navy duplex leaves bare edge or coral paint; white cotton suits navy or coral paint; soft-touch flood navy should get no edge paint unless proven by the printer. Foil gilding reads as flashy and sits close to the brand's no-gloss rule.

### Gaps
- No source compares square and rounded for perceived premium quality; all claims are vendor copy.
- No Indian-printer radius standards were found, and no die-cut thickness limit for 700 gsm or more.
