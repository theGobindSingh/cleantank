# Visual design of business cards that look expensive

Evidence note for the report writer. The web search tool returned mostly printer blogs and SEO pages. Pentagram, Brand New, Vignelli, Müller-Brockmann and Lupton searches gave no card-level detail, and I fetched no designer statement that explains small type or a specific layout rule. So the numeric rules below come from print vendors (4OVER4, Paperlust) and typographic references (Butterick), and the gallery examples are thin on rationale. Anything stated as a rule without a citation is in Inferences on purpose. Citations marked "search excerpt" or "search summary" come from the search tool's summary, and I did not open those pages, so treat the quote and its attribution as unconfirmed. Pages from geosirius.ifz.ru, internationaltrucks.com, domalberto.edu.br, primowater and git.mountainside.com were SEO filler and I discarded them, including a "64% of independent designers" figure attributed to AIGA that I could not trace.

## Hierarchy, white space, type size, tracking, alignment and information count

### Takeaway
No documented study or celebrated-card measurement gives a name-to-details ratio. Print guidance converges on a small name (about 10 to 12 pt) over 7 to 8 pt details, four levels at most, one alignment edge, and hierarchy made with size and space before weight and colour.

### Cited Findings
- Recommended sizes on a 90 x 55 mm card: name 9 to 11 pt (hard minimum 8), job title 7 to 9 pt, company 8 to 10 pt, phone and email 7 to 8 pt (minimum 6), postal address 6.5 to 7.5 pt, fine print 5.5 to 6 pt (minimum 5) — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477)
- A second vendor gives name 10 to 12 pt (11 in its "reliable set"), role 7 to 8 pt in tracked caps, contact 7 to 8 pt (7.5 in its example), a quiet item such as a URL at 6.5 pt, and a 7 pt floor for anything the reader must read. An oversized-name variant runs 18 to 24 pt — [4OVER4, Typographic business cards](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work)
- "11 next to 10 reads as a mistake, 11 next to 8 reads as a decision." Separate levels by "size and space first, weight second, color last", and use one accent colour, applied once — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work)
- Limit hierarchy to four levels: name, role, contact, one quiet item. Use one typeface in two or three weights; two families only with obvious contrast — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work)
- Use one alignment edge for the whole text block. Centring contact details under a left-aligned name is called the most common layout error in uploaded files — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work)
- Tracking: 8 pt caps opened 80 to 120 units so letters don't clot. Extreme tracking of 300 to 400 units on one line, once only. Contact block leading about 1.3 times the point size. Role-to-name gap 2 to 3 pt — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work)
- Always add 5 to 12% letterspacing to all caps and small caps. Lowercase doesn't ordinarily need it, though some typographers add space to lowercase under about 9 pt. Don't space letters so far apart that more letters would fit between them — [Butterick, Practical Typography](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html)
- Below 9 pt use Regular or Medium; keep Light for display at 10 pt and above; never Light or Thin in reversed type. Avoid condensed faces at 7 pt and below (8 pt floor for condensed); tall x-height faces hold up better — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477)
- Reversed type: add 1 to 1.5 pt to every size. Linen or cotton stock: add 0.5 to 1 pt, because of ink spread. Trim tolerance about ±0.5 mm, so keep text at least 3 mm from every edge — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477)
- Point size alone doesn't set apparent size: 10 pt Garamond reads noticeably smaller than 10 pt Helvetica — search summary of [Paperlust/4OVER4 guides](https://www.4over4.com/article/what-size-font-on-a-business-card) (not confirmed on a fetched page)
- Restraint read as skill: a type designer's letterpress cards "harness the power of minimalism and white space", conveying skill better than "something busy and flashy", and small light reversed type is called a "legibility minefield" — [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/inspiration/9-great-business-cards-for-type-and-lettering-designers) (search excerpt only; the full page would not load)
- A minimalist letterpress card keeps contact information in "relatively small type on the back" — [Card Observer](https://cardobserver.com/gallery/minimal-design-letterpress-business-card-for-an-interior-designer) (gallery note, search excerpt)
- Seegno's rebrand wanted a "clean, neutral brand", and got it from stock, print method, blind emboss and "contrasting letterpress" by Typoretum — [FPO](https://www.underconsideration.com/fpo/archives/2017/08/seegno-business-cards.php)
- The American Psycho cards are the pop reference for card status: type resembling Garamond on bone stock, with Silian Rail and Romalian Type invented for the novel — [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/american-psycho-business-card-scene/); [4OVER4](https://v2.4over4.com/article/the-american-psycho-business-card-and-why-it-still-works). Vendor identifications of the exact typeface conflict and are unverified.

### Inferences
- Name-to-details ratio from the vendor numbers: 11 over 7.5 pt is about 1.5:1. Treat 1.4 to 1.6:1 as the working band for the quiet variant. A jump to 18 to 24 pt (about 3:1) is the only other shape that reads as deliberate. Sizes between those two look undecided, by 4OVER4's logic.
- White space target: I found no cited percentage. A sensible design test is that the text block occupies under about half the face, with at least 5 mm margin on a 90 x 55 mm card (more than Paperlust's 3 mm safety minimum, which is a print limit and not a look).
- For this brand: name in Anek 600 at 11 pt; role and labels in IBM Plex Mono 500 caps at 6.5 to 7 pt with +0.08 to +0.10em (the site already uses these values in [docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md), and they sit within Butterick's range); contact in Anek 400 at 7.5 to 8 pt; leading about 1.3. No Light weights. Add Paperlust's 0.5 to 1 pt if the stock is cotton, and 1 to 1.5 pt on the navy side.

### Gaps
- No measured data on celebrated cards. I could not measure name size from images or find a designer stating one. The 1.5:1 figure is derived from vendor advice and is not an observed property of acclaimed cards.
- No designer or studio on record explaining why small type looks expensive. Thinking with Type's site failed to load, so no Lupton citation.
- The Vignelli search returned only that his IDCNY card used "Bodoni type with black and red colors" ([Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/idcny-the-numbers-don-t-lie-vignelli-massimo/_wHzKmRxMOGJyw?hl=en), search excerpt), which is an art piece and not a business card. Pentagram's cards turned up by name only ("On Rye", "Lord") with no layout detail.

## Front/back split and format

### Takeaway
Evidence is convention and opinion. Horizontal is the neutral default; vertical is read as creative. I found no source that sets a rule for flood-colour backs, but the examples pair a mark on one side with information on the other.

### Cited Findings
- Horizontal is the standard: "instantly recognizable, easy to scan" and suits almost any industry. Vertical is suggested for creative fields that want personality — [Mobilo](https://www.mobilocard.com/post/horizontal-vs-vertical-business-cards) (search excerpt; the summary did not tie the quote to one page, so check before quoting)
- No empirical work found on perception or memory of vertical versus horizontal physical cards. "Scanning" research found concerns digital UI cards — [UX Movement](https://uxmovement.com/content/vertical-vs-horizontal-cards-which-are-better/) (shown as an example of what the search returned; not applicable to print)
- Nosive Strukture (structural engineers, by Bunch): triplex card, three layers, with a black core between faces carrying logo and contact details, in a monochrome palette — [BP&O](https://bpando.org/2012/12/13/branding-nosive-strukture)
- Plan what goes on each side, and keep front and back coherent — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)
- Bare minimalist cards push contact details to the back in small type — [Card Observer](https://cardobserver.com/gallery/minimal-design-letterpress-business-card-for-an-interior-designer) (search excerpt)
- Build's Generation Press stationery used blue and white with blue card edges and blind-embossed paper, described as "stylish-stripped-back" — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/build-generation-press)

### Inferences
- Horizontal suits this client. "Spec sheet" is a landscape record format, Indian recipients are mostly filing a contact, and vertical's signal (personality) works against "calm". This is my reading of the brand plus the conventions above, not a sourced rule.
- Recommended split: white information side, navy (Primary 700) identity side with the wordmark only, or wordmark plus one mono reference line. It maps to the site's ratio of 60% neutral, 30% navy, at most 10% accent ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md)). Coral appears once across both sides, in line with 4OVER4's "one accent, applied once".
- Information on one side only keeps the identity side empty, which is where the expensive feel comes from. A second-language side (see the Indian norms section) would change this: then the navy side stops being empty, and that is a real trade-off.
- Coloured edges or a triplex core, as in Nosive Strukture and Generation Press, carry navy on a white-faced card without adding any print to the face.

### Gaps
- No source compares flood-colour backs with white ones, or front/back conventions in acclaimed work. Brand New's business card collection showed images with no text ([Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/collected/business_cards)).

## Conventions that read cheap

### Takeaway
Crowding, too many colours and fonts, and a QR code that merely repeats printed data are well attested. Icons and centring are contested: the icon argument comes from weak sources, and the centring evidence is specific to mixing alignments.

### Cited Findings
- Cramming is the headline error ("Kitchen Sink") — [Wave Connect](https://wavecnct.com/blogs/mistakes-when-designing-business-card); both too much and too little information are faults — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)
- Too many colours looks disorganised: keep to two or three, and a single colour can work if the layout is well planned. Too many font variations (fonts, bold, italics, line widths) to separate each detail is a named mistake — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)
- QR faults: broken links, linking to a site or number already printed, and use with audiences who aren't tech-savvy. Use it for what the card can't carry, and say where it leads — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)
- Centring: mixed alignment is the flagged error, not centring as such — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work). The American Psycho cards, a shorthand for status, are centred formal stacks, as I recall the film; no fetched source states it, so verify — [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/american-psycho-business-card-scene/)
- Icons: pages surfaced in search divide between "cluttered feels cheap" and "polished". All were low-quality SEO pages, so there is no citable designer authority on either side. The search's only firm note is that four icons (phone, cell, fax, WhatsApp) is a mess. I don't cite them as evidence.
- Hairlines and fine strokes break up on textured stock — [Paperlust](https://printshop.paperlust.co/blog?p=4477)
- Low-quality paper or printing makes a card look amateurish — [Wave Connect](https://wavecnct.com/blogs/mistakes-when-designing-business-card)
- Taglines, drop shadows and service bullet lists: not specifically addressed in any source I could fetch. Kimp's "remove details that add little value" is the nearest — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)

### Inferences
- For this brand, use mono text labels (T, M, E or TEL, MOB, MAIL) in place of icons. A labelled field looks like a spec sheet and removes the need for a pictogram. The site already uses mono caps labels and bans decorative motifs.
- Left-aligned on one edge is the right call here because the header rule is already "logo top-left (never centred)" ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md)), and not because centring is cheap in itself.
- Skip a QR code on the default card, or keep it very small on the back with a printed label naming the target. If WhatsApp booking matters, a labelled number does the same job with less risk.
- DESIGN.md already bans gradients, shadows, gloss, seals, shields and droplets, which removes most cheap-card clichés. The current logo in INFO-BANK (droplets, swoosh, gradient) conflicts with the card constraints. The report should say which mark the card uses.

### Gaps
- No credible source on drop shadows, taglines or service bullets as class signals. They are treated as cheap by assumption in the brief, and I found no evidence for or against.

## Industrial, engineering, laboratory, scientific-instrument and Swiss-style identities

### Takeaway
I found no documented spec-sheet or technical-label card from a named studio. The best example is Nosive Strukture. Swiss evidence is at the level of general principles, and the stronger models here are the record and label forms in the client's own references.

### Cited Findings
- Nosive Strukture by Bunch: "stark, technical identity", logotype of evenly spaced sans-serif letters with consistent line weights and square terminals, black and white, mix of heavy coated and uncoated stock, die-cut detail, frosted window decals. The technical feel is carried by structure and stock, not colour. A later portfolio adds colour on the covers and keeps the technical detail inside — [BP&O](https://bpando.org/2012/12/13/branding-nosive-strukture)
- AWAM Engineering & Design (Ashby Martin, self-promotion): laser-cut 165 gsm fold card using the typeface Isocteur, with an engineering feel from the mechanism. "I needed something innovative, it had to leave an impression". It is a novelty card, and the laser's burn marks had to be hidden with a thick black edge — [FPO](https://underconsideration.com/fpo/archives/2015/08/awam-engineering-design-business-cards.php)
- Multiconsult (engineering and architecture consultancy) redesign by EY Doberman was built on a typeface, Store Norske Ja — [Fonts In Use](https://www.fontsinuse.com/uses/70497/multiconsult-group) (search excerpt; no card detail)
- Monospace as an accent references technical environments; setting every line in mono is a frequent error because long strings read slowly — [fontpair](https://fontpair-o5.pages.dev/business-card-typography-for-software-companies-tech-startup-styles) (a low-authority page; this agrees with the point but I would not lean on it)
- Müller-Brockmann's SSB design manual exists as a documented Swiss corporate system — [People's Graphic Design Archive](https://peoplesgdarchive.org/item/17150/ssb-design-manual-joseph-mueller-brockmann). I found no card pages from it.
- Swiss style as usually described: strict grid, grotesque type, white space, asymmetry, a single accent on black and white — [Wikipedia](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Swiss_Style_(design)); [Developers Digest](https://www.developersdigest.tech/library/design/swiss-grid) (a web-oriented, secondary source)
- For systems, Richard Hollis's Swiss Graphic Design is the standard history — [Smithsonian catalogue](https://si.edu/object/swiss-graphic-design-origins-and-growth-international-style-1920-1965-richard-hollis%3Asiris_sil_905144). I have not read it for this note.

### Inferences
- The spec-sheet effect is cheap to build and needs no ornament: hairline rules, labelled fields in two columns, tabular figures, and a reference line such as `CT-CARD-01` or the ISO certificate number. It follows the site's mono reference tag pattern ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md)).
- Suggested grid for 90 x 55 mm: 5 mm margins, a two-column split at about one third (labels left, values right), and baseline steps of about 3.5 mm (7.5 pt at 1.3 leading is about 3.44 mm). Rules at 0.25 pt, navy, printed as a solid colour. These values are my arithmetic from the leading rule, not sourced. Confirm hairlines with the printer, since Paperlust warns on textured stock.
- Set values such as phone, email and reference in Plex Mono only if lines stay short. Long strings such as the email and address are better in Anek, keeping mono for labels and numbers, to avoid the all-mono error.
- A coral marker has a natural job on a report: one short rule or square beside the name, or one mark on a single field. 4OVER4's single-use accent supports it.
- "Lab report" works by restraint alone at this size. Too many fields make the card look like a form, so the card should imply a record rather than simulate one. Two ruled groups are probably enough.
- Mechanism cards like AWAM's cut against "calm" and should not be copied.

### Gaps
- No case found of a laboratory, instrument maker or testing house's cards with designer commentary. A search of Brand New for engineering, testing and inspection work returned none. Antalis "LAB" embossed cards turned up with no rationale ([visie.io](https://visie.io/media/antalis-598189)), and RightShip's Brand New card image has no accompanying text ([Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/archives/new_logo_and_identity_for_rightship_by_self_titled.php)).
- Teenage Engineering, Braun, Zeiss, Leica and similar references would be natural to check. I didn't verify any, so none should be cited as an example from this note.

## How much information a card should carry

### Takeaway
Four levels, only what the recipient acts on. ISO belongs on a card as a short exact text line with no logo. Registration numbers have no card rule and are a choice.

### Cited Findings
- Four levels at most — [4OVER4](https://www.4over4.com/article/typographic-business-cards-that-actually-work). Remove details that add little value — [Kimp](https://www.kimp.io/business-card-designs/)
- Certified companies "should use the certification body's logo, not the ISO logo", and "should not use the ISO logo to represent their certification status" — [ISO help](https://help.iso.org/en/articles/375997-can-i-use-the-iso-logo)
- Say the full standard, not "ISO certified"; a certifier's guide gives "ISO 9001:2015 Certified by [registrar]" as acceptable and rejects the undated "We are ISO 9001 certified" — search summary of [Hannon Consulting](https://hannonconsulting.co.nz/iso-certification/) and registrar guides such as [NQA](https://www.nqa.com/getattachment/clients/logos/NQA-USA-Logo-Guidance-Document.pdf?lang=en-ca) (excerpts; confirm against the specific document)
- Rule 18 of the CGST Rules requires the GSTIN on a name board at the place of business, and tax invoices must carry it. Nothing found extends this to visiting cards — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/display-gst-registration-certificate-gstin); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/penalty-for-non-display-of-gstin-on-board-incorrect-invoice-on-composition-dealers.html). Penalty figures conflict across these commentaries.
- Client facts ([INFO-BANK](../../../../ref/INFO-BANK.md)): the approved ISO wording is "ISO 9001:2015 certified (Q2A-UK Certification Board), Certificate No. Q2A-2026-0401T118011, valid to 31 March 2029". The words "accredited", "internationally recognised", "UKAS" and "IAF" are never used, since no accreditation body is claimed.

### Inferences
- Card content in priority order: name; designation; brand with its endorsement line (An Initiative of Gobind Galaxy); mobile; email; web; one office address. Then one small quiet line for ISO text. That is about 8 lines, which fits the four visual levels if contact fields form one block.
- ISO as a mono line: `ISO 9001:2015 CERTIFIED · Q2A-UK · CERT. NO. Q2A-2026-0401T118011` at about 6.5 pt on the back or foot. Omit the validity date on print, or decide with the owner: a card printed in 2026 with "valid to 31 March 2029" dates itself, yet the INFO-BANK "safe wording" includes it. This needs an owner decision. No seal or logo either way: DESIGN.md bans seals and fake marks, and ISO's own rule excludes the ISO logo.
- The short forms above shorten owner-approved wording, so check them against the INFO-BANK before print.
- GSTIN is useful to an accounts department and looks exact, so it suits the spec-sheet approach, but it is optional. The Udyam number, PAN and the registrations list (EPFO, ESI, BSNL) should stay off the card. PAN has no reason to be on it.
- The website's footer shows the ISO certificate and GSTIN together as a definition list, so a card can echo that one pattern at small scale.

### Gaps
- The GSTIN on file, `09LFS1473F5ZU`, has 13 characters and already stands flagged as faulty in [docs/research/pc-run/report.md](../../pc-run/report.md). Do not print it until the owner supplies the correct number.
- I couldn't read the Q2A certification terms, so I can't say whether the certifier's own mark is allowed or required, or whether its wording rules differ.
- ISO's own "Publicizing your ISO certification" page returned HTTP 403.

## Indian norms: recipients' expectations, Sikh names, second language, presentation and etiquette

### Takeaway
Paper cards remain culturally live in India, offered with both hands or the right hand, text facing the recipient. Degrees and rank are valued. The sources are mostly foreign-facing etiquette guides, and they disagree on whether Hindi is needed. For a Sikh name, "S." and "Sardar" are real forms, but the owner's preference decides and his approved form on file is "Mr."

### Cited Findings
- Receive with both hands or the right hand only; study the card, place it on the table, don't write on it unless asked; "If you have a university degree, include that information on the card" — [The Cultural Atlas](https://theculturalatlas.org/indian-culture/indian-culture-business-culture)
- "Workplaces in India are hierarchical, based on age and position", and the highest-ranking person makes final decisions — [The Cultural Atlas](https://theculturalatlas.org/indian-culture/indian-culture-business-culture)
- Receive with a brief glance rather than putting it away immediately, and address colleagues by professional title and surname unless invited otherwise — [World Travel Guide](https://www.worldtravelguide.net/guides/asia/india/business-communications)
- Present with text facing the recipient. Sources conflict on Hindi: one says cards should carry the native language and Hindi, one language per side, another says translation isn't needed because English is widely spoken in business — search summary of [CIBTvisas](https://cibtvisas.com/blog/business-etiquette-india), [Bowling Green State University](https://blogs.bgsu.edu/culturaldosanddonts/india-cultural-profile/) and similar guides (excerpts; attribution of each claim to each page not checked)
- Indian HR event report (People Matters TechHR India 2025): cards are "deeply cultural", a card handed over with both hands conveys respect, and offering one to senior leaders "remains a mark of grace". It describes a CHRO with a matte-laminated bilingual English and Hindi card. Advice is to carry both a card and a digital option. No statistics — [People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/amp/article/business/qr-code-or-classic-card-how-are-you-swapping-contacts-this-year-45770)
- "Sardarji" is "abbreviated S." and is an honorific similar to "Mister" — [Onomastikon](https://tekeli.li/onomastikon/India/Sikh/Names.html). Sardar is customary in place of "Mr." before a Sikh name, placed before the name — [Wikipedia, Sikh titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_titles). The feminine is Sardarni — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_titles)
- Not uncontested: SGPC says that since independence a policy discourages the courtesy title, with Sikhs "invariably referred to as a Shri in official" language — [SGPC](https://sgpc.net/sardar/). SikhiWiki records an objection on Gurbani grounds — [SikhiWiki](https://www.sikhiwiki.org/index.php/Sardar). Nihang Sikhs don't use it — search summary of [Sikh titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_titles) (excerpt)
- Gurmukhi is the script for Indian Punjabi; Shahmukhi is mainly Pakistani — search excerpt from [OpenStreetMap wiki](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/Key:name:pa). Indian institutional tenders ask for dual-language cards — [Spices Board tender](https://indianspices.com/indianspices/hin/sites/default/files/Tender_VISITING%20CARD.pdf) (excerpt)
- Client facts ([INFO-BANK](../../../../ref/INFO-BANK.md)): Mr. Gurdev Singh is Proprietor of Gobind Galaxy and Founder & CEO of Clean Tank Services, signs "G. Singh", and is also written "Mr G D Singh". The regd. office is in Kanpur; the correspondence and sales office is in Kharar, Punjab, with separate phone numbers. The site shows only the correspondence office by owner's call ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md)).

### Inferences
- A name on a card generally goes without an honorific, because honorifics are what other people use. Printing "Gurdev Singh" alone is the safest and quietest form, and the mono designation below carries rank. "S. Gurdev Singh" is defensible if the owner wants it, and it reads as a Punjab-specific sign of respect. This is the owner's decision. I found no style source for it.
- If the owner likes the honorific: "S." on an English side, "ਸ." or "ਸ੍ਰ." on a Gurmukhi side. The Gurmukhi abbreviation is from my own knowledge and is unsourced, so a native reader must confirm it.
- Designation: "Founder & CEO" is what the recipient reads for rank, and hierarchy-minded readers want it visible. "Proprietor" is the legal role and could sit beside Gobind Galaxy, if used at all.
- Degrees: the sources lean towards printing them, but INFO-BANK lists none, and "35 years" is a claim, not a credential. Don't add any to fill the slot.
- Second language: given the conflict in sources, make it a decision and not a default. English alone gives the best chance of a clean, empty navy side. For Punjabi-speaking and government or institutional buyers, a Hindi or Punjabi back has real value but needs Anek Devanagari or Gurmukhi at a larger size, as Indic scripts generally carry more strokes and matras. A practical way out: a default English card with a navy back, and a smaller run with a Hindi or Punjabi back for field or government use. Don't print three languages on one card.
- Address: one card should carry one office and its phone, matching the site. If the Kanpur office matters for formal or government buyers, a separate run is cleaner than two addresses on one side. Which office and number go on the card needs the owner's call.
- Number format: the site's "+91 94535 17514" form should carry over.

### Gaps
- No Indian-authored etiquette source or survey of what Indian B2B recipients expect (mobile, address, designation, GST). Expectation of mobile and office address on cards is my general understanding, unsourced. The etiquette guides are written for foreign visitors, and some are weak.
- No Punjab-specific card etiquette found, and no Sikh style guide on "S." in print. Whether The Tribune or Punjabi dailies use "S." was not established.
- No evidence on whether a Hindi or Punjabi back improves response.
- Regional differences within India not found.

## Examples of well-regarded Indian or service-industry cards

### Takeaway
I found no acclaimed Indian card case study and no acclaimed card for a cleaning or water-services firm. The usable Indian leads are multilingual type designers and matched-script typefaces.

### Cited Findings
- Sarang Kulkarni's Mumbai studio built a niche in multilingual branding, with clients including Vodafone, Star and Pidilite — [Typoday](https://typoday.in/2016/sarang.html)
- Avani Phansalkar designs identities and typefaces for multilingual contexts, with a focus on Devanagari and Latin — [It's Nice That, Ones to Watch 2026](https://www.itsnicethat.com/ones-to-watch-2026-showcase/avani-phansalkar)
- Studio Eksaat's Khoj International identity is listed in the Kyoorius awards, with no system detail — [Kyoorius](https://awards.kyoorius.com/2016/design/in-book-winners.html)
- Begum Devanagari (Indian Type Foundry) pairs matching serif Devanagari and Latin — [MyFonts](https://www.myfonts.com/de/collections/begum-devanagari-variable-font-indian-type-foundry)
- "We rarely see Indic scripts in professional design" — [CreativePro](https://creativepro.com/?p=127356) (search excerpt)
- Service-adjacent: Generation Press, a printer, by Build with Colophon's Poynings Stencil — [It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/build-generation-press)

### Inferences
- The client already has the matched-script answer in Anek Latin, Devanagari and Gurmukhi ([docs/DESIGN.md](../../../../DESIGN.md)), so a bilingual card doesn't need a new pairing. Plex Mono has no Indic glyphs, so Indic labels fall back to Anek.
