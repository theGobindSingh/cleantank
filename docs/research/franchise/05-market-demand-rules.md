### 1.2 Things that are NOT established (do not put on the page)

- **A national rule that tanks must be cleaned every six months.** Not found. CPHEEO manuals cover utility service reservoirs (drain, scrub, disinfect) and talk of "periodic" cleaning, with no household interval found. National Building Code: no interval found. The "every 6 months" rhythm is trade advice ([studiomatrx guide](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/water-tank-cleaning-india)), not law.
- **A municipal bye-law in Chandigarh, Mohali or Panchkula with a fixed interval and fine.** Not found. Municipal acts put a general duty on owners to keep storage "in a thoroughly clean condition" ([example](https://www.ielrc.org/content/e8806.pdf)). The Mohali 2024 order (F2) was an emergency order, not a standing rule.
- **Any tank-cleaning market size for India.** None credible. Report-mill numbers are for tank sales or purifier servicing, not cleaning. Franchise India's own articles are promotional ("wide gap between demand and supply", [2017](https://franchiseindia.com/insights/en/article/tank-full-of-opportunities.36063)).
- **Number of households with overhead tanks.** Census 2011 and NSS do not report it as far as I could find.
- **Jal Jeevan Mission or Swachh Bharat requiring tank cleaning.** They do not. JJM is about tap connections and water quality testing. Tap connections do not remove storage where supply is intermittent [inference].
- **"Drinking water tanks are the only thing consumed directly from storage"** (our INFO-BANK print line). Rhetorical; not a citable fact.

### 1.3 Reading for the page [inference]

The honest pitch is local and institutional: Tricity has had tank-linked outbreaks with official orders; food businesses, hospitals and schools have documented reasons to keep cleaning records; 24x7 supply that would remove household tanks is years away in Chandigarh. Avoid national market-size claims.
