# Tricity recheck confirms prices, shrinks the rival field

> Later evidence: [the PC run](../pc-run/report.md) read Google Search from Kharar and changes some claims here, mainly Google and Meta ads, the Dera Bassi and New Chandigarh Maps leaders, sized rate cards and language switchers. Where they differ, the PC run is newer.

The recheck moved the picture of who is competing far more than the picture of what things cost. Welco's Sponsored Maps slot did not appear once in 98 searches, Fixomes is the only Tricity-serving firm seen with a Google tank ad naming the Tricity, and the Meta field may be just two operators. Urban Company's ₹949 is confirmed live in five towns, its upper bands now read ₹1,149 and ₹1,899, and every Guga figure held, so Clean Tank's ₹944 and ₹1,180 with GST stay about level with Urban Company at worst. Google now lists Punjabi for AI Mode, AI Overviews and Gemini, and Business Profile search terms need neither the API nor a 60-day wait. Kurali and Ropar, blank last time, show no tank cleaner's listing nearby, while Clean Tank itself still has no Maps listing on any of 20 name, phone and address loads. All of this came from a browser set to Tricity coordinates on a server whose IP stayed in Mumbai, on Saturday 31 October 2026. That makes it good evidence for published pages and for Maps in five towns, and weak evidence for search. Google Search, Justdial, IndiaMART, Perplexity and ChatGPT went unread, the other engines blocked within minutes, and about 29 of 30 planned queries have no valid data. Nobody was phoned, so every price here is a listed price.

Ranks do not simply follow distance, and with review counts hidden the cause cannot be shown. Reviews are the obvious candidate. In Kharar, Clean Tank's home market, A-One led every query it appeared on, and A-One, Shree Balaji and Wazara hold the top three.

The polluted rows come from Navi Mumbai and Thane firms, which take no slots where local supply is dense and flood in where it is thin. That pattern points to the Mumbai IP, so those ranks should be read as an artefact. Adding the town name and coordinates cleared Kurali's top 14 of Mumbai rows ([Kurali](https://www.google.com/maps/search/water+tank+cleaning+Kurali/@30.8319,76.575,13z?hl=en&gl=in)), and that is where the real finding sits: Maps showed no listing categorised as a tank cleaner in either town. It says nothing about firms working there without a listing. Bare and town-name queries also produce different leaders, so a firm needs both proximity and the town's name in its signals ([Kharar](https://www.google.com/maps/search/water+tank+cleaning+service+Kharar)).

Star ratings separate nobody: 33 of 39 Tricity listings are 4.8 or above. Review counts would, but Maps hid them on 90 of 91 listing pages (the exception was a gurudwara), and among tank firms only Welco's count could be read. The 50 Welco reviews visible on its [own paid profile](https://business.google.com/v/welco-tank-cleaning-services/05022997511415029701/7d5e/_/rev) talk about punctuality (25 of 50), staff (13), hygiene (11) and neatness (10). All four mentions of chemicals were favourable, one reviewer thought ₹1,500 was high, and one complained about water drained without warning and a broken tank ball. The sample is one firm's own page, with some sentences repeated under different names, so it says more about what customers notice than about Welco. Nothing from it belongs on the site.

Paid competition is smaller than it looked. Google's Transparency Center gives the same counts as before (Fixomes 24, Busy Bucket 5, Smart Cleaning 15, Tricity Cleaners 11, Safaiwale 28), but ad text separates them. [Fixomes](https://adstransparency.google.com/?region=IN&domain=fixomes.com) runs "Tank Deep Cleaning Services In Chandigarh Tricity" with same-day service and a rating. The others seen sell home cleaning or target Delhi. Only part of those 83 ads was read, so this is a reading of samples. On Meta, 94 unique ads gave four advertiser names and five Tricity tank ads, none new: [Good Life Future](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=26586607767617468), two from GLF, AR, and the ₹399 [Water Tank Cleaning Service](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1407923478165926). Their messages are "Govt. Registered" and price. Good Life Future's website returned HTTP 500 on both checks that day ([site](https://goodlifefuture.in/)).

The on-site openings survive rendering. JavaScript changed nothing, no tank page in the set carries Hindi or Punjabi, none has hreflang or a language switcher, and few pair a working WhatsApp link with a tank price (Grace, KPS and Mr Service Expert did). The live old homepage at [cleantankservices.com](https://www.cleantankservices.com/) names Kharar and Kurali only in the office address, with no price and no Hindi or Punjabi. The brand name is exposed too: searching "Clean Tank Services" from Kharar returns A-One first, and "Gobind Galaxy" resolves to an electronics shop ([Maps](https://www.google.com/maps/search/Gobind+Galaxy/@30.7464,76.6456,13z?hl=en&gl=in)). The site rules still apply to anything built on this. Good Life Future's "not local laborers" line stays off Clean Tank's copy, rivals are not named, and their "99.99%", "WHO-approved" and "Govt. Certified" claims are not matched. Clean Tank's own paperwork is the answer to them.

One housekeeping item is resolved. The US search tool returned a GitHub Pages copy of an old Clean Tank site ahead of the live domain, with its summary quoting "India's Most Trusted". A direct check today shows the [Pages root](https://thegobindsingh.github.io/cleantank/) is now a blank Next.js starter template and`/about-us` returns 404, so those claims are off the live page. Search indexes may keep the old text for a while. The [repository](https://github.com/theGobindSingh/cleantank) behind it is still public.

### Institutions buy in public, but award prices stay locked

Panjab University is the clearest new buyer: ₹3,40,000 for underground tanks and ₹4,75,000 for overhead tanks in houses in 2024, each re-advertised at the same estimate, the first four times and the second three times between June and October ([underground](https://tenders.puchd.ac.in/tenders/2024/20241029105614-cleaningofundergroundwatertanksofv.pdf), [overhead](https://tenders.puchd.ac.in/tenders/2024/20241029105356-cleanignofwatertanks-houses.pdf)). The repeats suggest failed or rejected bids, though no document says so. Its notice wants contractors on the approved lists of PWD, CPWD, MES, boards, HUDA, PUDA, CHB and similar bodies. The owner should check whether Clean Tank's listed registrations qualify, since BSNL enlistment is not named.

[NABI's notice](https://nabi.res.in/backend/web/img/tenders/tenders-619-Tendernotice_110526.pdf) confirms ₹5,70,896 for three years inclusive of taxes, which is 12 cleans at about ₹47,575 each, and a text search found no ISO or NABL requirement. Other recent items: Chandigarh Administration's overhead and underground work at the [High Court](https://tender18.com/tenders-details/15948422), about ₹1 lakh; [Jandpur raw water tank](https://www.tenderdetail.com/Indian-Tenders/TenderNotice/58245353/23ea1004201e3eab6379b3bfb4d7c33f) at ₹3.83 lakh, closing 4 November; and [MES 3 BRD](https://www.tendershark.com/details/chandigarh-tender/engineer-in-chief-branch-military-engineer-services/d20bc2ff-7713-49a0-8c44-de03d12dce26) at ₹35 lakh composite, closing today. [CCET](https://www.tendershark.com/details/chandigarh-tender/administration/3292226b-dd9b-47f4-8242-d44ef3b442d1) at ₹2,63,738 remains the only Tricity award with a named winner, 18.9% under estimate. Nothing surfaced for PEC, GMCH-32, GMADA, PGIMER, NIPER, CHB, ISB or MC Mohali, though several of those checks covered current lists only.

Per-unit evidence is unchanged: [CUP's July 2026 estimate](https://cup.edu.in/sites/default/files/NITWTC.pdf) of ₹82.55 per sqm and ₹0.48 and ₹0.35 per litre is still the nearest. Punjab's [CSR-2020](https://pwd.punjab.gov.in/UploadDocs/CSR%202020/csr2020.pdf) has no tank-cleaning item in its searchable text, so Punjab buyers borrow from the Delhi rate book or use lump estimates. No Tricity society, hotel or hospital rate is public anywhere. INFO-BANK contract values stay internal and are left out of this report.

The compliance position is now clearer and more limited. No standing Chandigarh, Punjab or Mohali rule setting a cleaning interval was found. FSSAI's "cleaned periodically" with a register ([FSSAI](https://fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/regulations/Compendium_Licensing_Regulations_04_08_2021.pdf)) binds food businesses only. The 2024 Mohali order can be cited only as history. It is still the strongest local fact available, because an [FIR against two PG owners](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/amid-cholera-outbreak-in-mohalis-kumbhra-fir-registered-against-2-pg-owners-for-violating-orders-643871) followed it during the cholera outbreak, and it speaks to PG, hostel and rental owners. Only press reports were read, so check the order's wording before quoting it.

The Business Profile changes work in favour of the owner's timing. The old argument for verifying early was a 60-day clock on keyword data. That clock applies only to an API nobody here needs, and search terms arrive monthly once a profile is verified ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9918094?hl=en-IN)). Finishing after launch therefore costs reviews and brand-search capture, and costs no keyword data. Search Console should still come first, because domain verification may verify the profile instantly, though nobody has tested that on a service-area business ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en-IN)). Google lists cleaning services as a service-area example and allows up to 20 areas, set by city or postcode with no radius, within about two hours' drive, so the Tricity and Kurali to Ropar all fit ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en-IN)). Its [guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en-IN) confirm hiding the address and rule out virtual offices. No India-specific rule turned up on the pages compared. The evidence on naming points to "Clean Tank Services" alone, with the endorsement line kept out of the business name.

Punjabi's listing removes the case for treating those pages as invisible to AI, but it says nothing about quality. A language list shows the product accepts Punjabi. It does not show how often answers trigger, what they cite, or how Gurmukhi fares against Roman script, so the hand test stays and now has a firmer reason. No study of Hindi or Punjabi citations exists. Autocomplete points the same way as before: phone-number intent in Hinglish and Hindi ("pani ki tanki saaf karne wale ka number"), "price list" in English for Chandigarh, Mohali and Kharar, "commercial" for Mohali and Panchkula, generic Hindi leaning DIY, almost nothing in Gurmukhi, and AMC barely registering ([test query](https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&hl=hi&gl=in&oe=utf8&q=pani%20ki%20tanki%20saaf)). These are suggestions, not volumes, and they came from a Mumbai IP. The output listed Nagpur and Pune.

The evidence for AEO choices held with some caveats. llms.txt has no link to citations across about 300,000 domains ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/llms-txt/)), and separately 97% of about 38,000 files got no requests in a month ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/)), a sample of Ahrefs users. The brand correlation of 0.664 against 0.218 dates from May 2025 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/)). The only Indian test still has Justdial in 25 of 48 answers ([Nico Digital](https://www.nicodigital.com/seo/ai-engines-local-business-search-test/)). ChatGPT holds 77.32% of India's measured AI referrals and Gemini 16.01% ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/ai-chatbot-market-share/all/india)). The line that engines "lift direct answers" has no source, and Google says chunking isn't required ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)), so that sentence in`docs/AEO-GEO.md` needs softening. No confirmed ranking update landed in October. Google's 30 October post on a shared AI training control fits the all-crawlers-allowed decision: a blanket training opt-out would count against Google-Extended unless it is explicitly allowed, and Gemini's grounded citations depend on that ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/10/ai-training-control)). Guides stay deferred, and nothing here argues for moving them up.

## What this round cannot prove, and what the PC run must fill

A browser location does not move an IP. Meta rendered in Marathi from Chandigarh coordinates, the same as the earlier Mumbai run, and Mumbai firms flooded the thin towns, so at least some services place this browser by IP. Urban Company did resolve to its Chandigarh city, and Maps honoured coordinates in dense towns, which is why those two sets of findings are the strongest here. Everything else about what a Tricity resident sees remains untested. Maps was also desktop, signed out and in "limited view", read once on one Saturday. A 30-minute re-run kept 9 or 10 of 10 names but moved several positions, so ranks are "was present" and no more. Absences are the weakest claims in this report: no Sponsored rows may only mean this browser was not served one, no listing for Clean Tank could miss a pending or hidden-address profile, and no Tricity ad from a firm may mean the ad went unread.

Search visibility is nearly a blank. Bing gave one valid set before serving off-topic pages, DuckDuckGo, Brave, Yandex and Mojeek blocked on the first load, and Bing Maps, Bing AI Mode and Copilot were not attempted once Bing degraded. Google Search and Justdial, along with Perplexity and ChatGPT, had refused this server's automated browser in earlier runs, so this round left them alone, and IndiaMART was left out too. Trends gave no data. The cause of the Bing degradation cannot be separated from other scripts on the same IP that afternoon.

## Conclusion

The earlier work read a crowded market in which rivals were buying attention. This round suggests that much of the attention is earned on Maps by small firms with no websites, and that few firms are paying for tank searches here at all. That makes the decision to spend nothing cheaper than it looked, since paid search is not where Clean Tank is being outrun. The open cost is time without a listing: until the profile goes live, a Kharar search for Clean Tank's own name lands on A-One.

The market's weakness is also more specific now. Only two public sized cards can be trusted locally, and no competitor tank page checked carries Hindi or Punjabi. A published price in three languages is therefore rarer than the earlier study implied, and Kurali and Ropar add two Maps positions with no local holder. The next evidence that can change this picture is local, and most of it takes a person a few minutes each: a signed-in Maps pass, a booking page, a handful of calls.
